# Chapter 9

## Celeste

[Quiet, reflective narration] Lauren came back a fortnight later, on a warm Tuesday morning. Tuesdays were the days Mara pretended she had time. She didn’t, of course. Mara never did. But Tuesdays were when she scheduled those "need-to-get-done" tasks. The cutting table was clear, the mannequin was dressed in half a bodice, and the new jacket prototype sat like a dare. Lauren stepped in with nothing but a flat folder under her arm and a tote on her shoulder. She looked neat, composed, and slightly sharpened around the edges. 
Mara glanced up.
[Drily] “What now,” she said drily.
Lauren didn’t waste time warming the air.
[Clearly] “His father—"
She stopped. Mara’s mouth tightened.
[Clearly] “I told him Charlie had made other plans,” Lauren said. “Roger didn't take it well.”
Mara’s eyes flicked to the folder.
“What’s that?”
Lauren placed the folder on the corner of the worktable, away from fabric, and removed the contents — an envelope and a printed sheet — with the same neatness Charlie arranged paper on school desks. The envelope was plain and official-looking. Lauren didn’t open it, but left it on the table like something scraped off a shoe.
She slid the printed sheet forward.
On it was a photograph of a Faire actress wearing the new jacket prototype. Her arms were raised in a dramatic pose, the kind that usually tore seams under the arm and split closures at the waist. The jacket had clearly held: clean line, no gaping, no strain.
Below the image Lauren had typed a legend:
**Previous issue:** underarm seam tearing after repeated movement;  **Change:** reinforced gusset + eased sleeve head + seam tape at stress line; **Result:** 3 full shifts; no tear; improved comfort; faster dressing; **Notes:** closure placement adjusted for quick change; no snagging

Nothing was left to trust. Everything was there to verify.
Mara studied the photo for a moment before motioning me over.
[Dry] “Signal,” Lauren said, glancing at Mara's face. Then she indicated the envelope with her thumb.
[Dry] “And that,” she added, “is noise.”
Mara’s mouth twitched as her eyebrows rose slightly — almost amused, almost approving.
“You took the picture?”
[Modestly] "I had a free afternoon last week," Lauren said with a shrug, "so I did a tour of the Faire. I noticed your prototype."
Across the room, Charlie was at the side bench, pinning a lining into a bodice piece. He hadn’t looked up when Lauren entered. But he did look now. Not to the photo at first. To the envelope.
At the sight of the official paper, his hands slowed. A pin hovered between his fingers. 
Lauren noticed without turning. 
Mothers always did.
[Quietly] “Keep your hands,” she said quietly.
Charlie blinked. “But—”
[Clearly] “No but,” his mum replied. No cruelty in it, just blunt care. “You’re not in trouble.”
He swallowed and slowly went back to pinning.
Mara picked up the sheet and examined it.
“This,” Mara said, tapping the list, “is useful.”
Lauren allowed herself a thin, purposeful smile. Mara’s gaze slid to the envelope.
“And that.”
Lauren’s brows twitched.
[Clearly] “Roger wants to know what trade he'll finish with,” she said. “He wants ‘proof’.”
Mara’s eyes narrowed.
“What does Charlie want?”
[Puzzled] "I'm not entirely certain," Lauren replied.
Mara eyed her.
[Clearly] "Do you think you should talk it over with him?"
[Softly] "We did talk a bit, last night," she replied.
"And?"
Lauren let out her breath in a puff with a frown.
[Resigned] "Well, his father was there."
Mara nodded, then tipped her head.
[Clearly] "The tearoom's free."
Lauren picked up her tote.
"Charlie." Her voice was gentle.
The two went into the tearoom. I could feel Mara's eyes on me.
She didn't need to say anything.
[Softly] Their decision.
Mara joined Lucy at the fitting curtain while I opened windows to let out some of the steam. The air outside was almost as warm, but drier, seasoned with diesel fumes mixed with hot chips soaking in re-used cooking oil and the occasional reminder that port-a-potties were just around the corner.
When they emerged just before lunch, Charlie looked solemn; and Lauren looked relieved but also filled with intent. She went straight to the worktable and opened the envelope.
Mara approached slowly. Her expression was implacable, but her eyes searched Lauren's face.
"And?"
[Gently] "He's determined to make a go of it."
Mara's eyebrows barely twitched.
"And so am I," Lauren added.


Mara gave a single nod, staring at the envelope.
[Clearly] "I take it this is for me."
[Calmly] "It is." Lauren pulled out a folded document. “Charlie  wants to stay here to learn the trade. I'm happy that there is a future in it. *For him.* This is real.”
She handed Mara the document.
Mara unfolded the paper carefully.
[Clearly] “You are asking for commitment.”
Lauren met her gaze.
[Drily] “I am.” 
She paused.
[Clearly] “I need to be able to show that this is real," she said, her voice showing a hint of vulnerability, as if reluctant to make her need into Mara's burden. "That Charlie’s training for a trade. With standards.”
Mara’s gaze held hers.
“A contract.”
Lauren didn’t flinch. 
[Clearly] “Yes.” 
She took in a deep breath. [Clearly] “A contract will turn noise into signal," she added. "Work placement language. Attendance logs." Her mouth tightened. "Roger wants a form, I’ll give him a form. You handle standards. I handle noise.”
Mara removed the chalk pencil from the top of her ear. 
She looked across the room at Charlie, who had resumed pinning, slower than before but steady.
Then, her eyes returned to Lauren.
“You’re asking for a structure.”
[Clearly] “Exactly.”
Mara exhaled through her nose.
[Calmly] “Fine,” Mara said. “Here’s the structure. Charlie shows up every day, full-time. He keeps hours. He logs tasks. He does training modules I set. He gets evaluated like all my staff. If he fails, he fails. No protection from anyone.”
Lauren’s voice didn’t soften. 
This was the contract.
“Agreed.”
Charlie looked up then — finally, his gaze flicking from his mum to Mara, and then, finally, to me.
“Charlie,” Mara said.
Charlie stood quickly.
[Gently] “Yes Mara?”
Mara held up the printed sheet.
[Clearly] “Your mother has a costume testing plan, with receipts,” Mara said. “She's also asking me for a contract so that you can learn the trade. We will do this properly.”
Charlie stared at the testing plan.
"I need to know your decision."
He looked up.
[Gently] "I want to learn the trade, Mara."
[Clearly] “Right then,” she said, her tone clipped, and began to study the document.
[Calmly] “I’m not asking for anything," Lauren said, "but for you to stay— *visible*.”
Charlie blinked at the word 'visible'.
[Clearly] “Visible,” Mara agreed, “means you write down what you do. You show up on time. You finish tasks. *Visibly*.”
Charlie’s throat dipped.
[Gently] “I can do that.”
“Good,” Mara said, and turned briskly back to the mannequin. “You can start now.”
She gestured at the prototype jacket.
“We solved the tear,” Mara said. “Now I want the pull solved without it *fighting*.”
She ran her thin fingers over the seam.
“It needs to survive heat and tourists and people stupidly grabbing sleeves,” Mara continued. “But do it without adding bulk that ruins the silhouette.”
She eyed him closely.
[Clearly] “Tell me where it fails.”
Charlie stepped closer to the mannequin. He lifted the sleeve gently and pressed the seam line with his fingertips. His eyes focused. He only hesitated for a moment.
[Quietly] “It’s not the seam, Mara,” he said quietly. “It’s the angle. The gusset’s correct, but the sleeve head is fighting it. You need two millimetres more ease, here." He pointed without touching. "The tape needs to stop before the pivot point, not run through it.”
Mara’s face changed.
[Clearly] “That,” Mara said, “is an answer.”
Of course it was.
Lauren watched him. I felt something click into place.
Mara didn't look up. 
[Clearly] "I'll have a signed copy of a work contract ready for you in a couple of days."
Lauren picked up her tote, nodded once at me — not warmth, exactly, but acknowledgement — and moved to the door. As she left, she paused and looked back at Mara.
“Thank you,” she said.
Mara didn’t look up from the sleeve.
[Clearly] “Now, it's up to Charlie,” she said.
[Calmly] “Yes, it is.”
The door clicked shut.
