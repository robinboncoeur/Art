# Chapter 10

## Celeste

[clearly] “Look at this.”
Mara slid a thin archival print-out across the cutting table. The image was a plate from an old catalogue: eighteenth century, late enough that it carried a Georgian neatness, early enough that it still remembered softness. A woman’s garment, fitted through the back, generous through the skirt, closures designed for working hands. 
It had been designed by necessity and for function. It had intelligence in it.
My pulse quickened.
“This isn’t a costume,” I said. “It’s *equipment*.”
Mara’s lips were a thin line of approval.
[clearly] “It’s clever,” she said. “See the reinforcement here? And here.”
I leaned in. The sketch suggested a hidden strength at stress points: underarm, waist, the place where movement always found the weak seam.
“I can draft this.”
Mara handed me a pencil.
[clearly] “Then do it.”
That was Mara. The moment you said it, you owned it.
We wordlessly split the labour. 
I took the research: proportions, plausible fabric weight, seam placement, what could be original and what had to be translated for a modern body in a modern job. 
Mara took the pattern: chalk, ruler, critical decisions.
Charlie kept to the repair pile, but his darting glances in our direction betrayed his interest.
We moved fast. Paper became pattern. Pattern became cloth. Cloth became the first prototype under Mara’s hands. 
The room filled with that particular concentration that only happens when a thing becomes real: pins tapping into the pincushion, the soft rasp of shears, the hiss of the iron. By mid-afternoon the garment hung from the mannequin, half-finished but already legible. 
Even unfinished, it had a line. It didn’t scream 'pretty', but rather whispered 'capable'.
Mara stepped back, eyes narrowed.
[clearly] “It’s got spine.”
“And purpose,” I replied.
Charlie looked at the garment from the other side of the room. He quietly rose and, biting his lower lip, approached the mannequin. After studying it for a moment, he reached in and adjusted a seam allowance that had curled under itself. 
Mara noticed. Mara always noticed.
[clearly] “Already adjusting things, Charlie?”
He paused with his fingers on the fabric.
[meekly] “Just— keeping it clean.”
I noticed then his hair had been brushed back more carefully than usual, and his t-shirt had been pressed.
We didn’t take any more time to admire our prototype. We did what we always did with any garment we'd improved: we tested with a chemise and a real day's work.
On Lucy.
Lucy was our default tester. A fitter and a tester, she was the one we relied on to give us real-world information on a garment. She would do Faire actress work at times: lifting baskets, crouching for children, running for late arrivals.
She was the kind of wearer who put costumes through their paces. 
She came back near closing time that evening, cheeks flushed, hair escaping pins.
[breezily] “Got lots of compliments. It looks brilliant,” she announced, breezily. “But—"
She turned slightly and tugged at the underarm.
“Here,” she said. “When I lift my arms. It’s not tearing, but it’s like it’s fighting me.”
Mara’s eyes flicked to the seam line.
[grunting] “And *this*,” Lucy added, touching the waist closure, “I don't know that I'd want to wear it another day, to be honest. Look, it held, but it got tugged a lot. People grab. You know.”
Yes. We knew.
Mara took the garment from Lucy the focused way a mechanic takes a part off an engine and laid it flat on the table. She pressed her palm along the seam.
[clearly] “This is not failure,” Mara said to me. “Still, I don't want Lucy wearing it again until we get some core issues sorted.”
"How do you suggest we sort the issues?”
[clearly] "Just having Lucy tell us where a garment pulls is not enough information." She ran her fingers along the seam. "We need to know what a garment is telling us about the physics.”
Mara turned the dress over.
[clearly] "The 'where' isn't the question. That bit's as plain as daylight."
"So, do we run a seam this way or that, a few times?"
She shook her head, and turned towards the mending table.
[clearly] "No, I need the 'how'. I want the physics." 
I felt my spine stiffen.
[clearly] “Charlie.”
The way Mara said 'Charlie' at that moment landed differently than 'Rossignol' ever did.
Charlie looked up. Mara held up the garment with two fingers.
[clearly] “You,” Mara said, her voice flat, “are going to test this dress, Charlie.”
Lucy blinked. [teasing] “*He* is?”
Mara nodded. [clearly] “Yes. *He* is.”
The logic was undeniable. 
[soft, firm] Physics. 
Charlie could read a behaviour and know where the fix lived.
I carefully watched his face. The smallest frown slowly appeared as his eyes dropped to his torso — doing maths. 
A calculation: [soft] will this even sit right?
His eyes went to the dress again and his frown melted into slight confusion.
Mara silently waited.


I watched Lucy casually sidle up beside him.
[teasing] “Well,” she murmured innocently, eyes bright, “a tester! And of a dress.”
Charlie glanced at her and gave one shoulder a nearly imperceptible shrug.
His mouth went crooked. 
[meekly] “It wouldn’t have been my first choice.”
Mara didn’t react at all.
[drily] “Noted,” she said. “And irrelevant.”
Charlie looked at the dress again.
[meekly] “So you don't need me to tell you where it pulls.”
[clearly] “I already know where it pulls.”
His eyes lifted to hers.
[meekly] “You want: *why*.”
“Exactly.”
We moved to the fitting area. Mara drew the curtain and held the garment up.
[clearly] “Arms up.”
Charlie complied. Mara worked quickly, checking lines, pull, and where the fabric resisted movement.
[clearly] “Turn. Now lift your arms. Higher. Good. Twist. Again.”
And then, her voice: irritated yet satisfied.
[clearly] “There,” she said. “Feel that?”
Charlie’s voice came through the curtain.
[meekly] “Yes,” he said. “It’s not the seam. It’s the *direction* of strain. When I raise my arms, the tension line runs across the tape and stops the fabric doing its job.”
Mara's exhale was almost harsh. 
[clearly] “Say it again.”
I stood just outside the fitting area curtain, notebook in hand, scribbling furiously.
[meekly] “The closure,” he continued, “holds. But if someone grabs here—" 
The curtain moved slightly.
[meekly] “—it transfers force to the waistband. The reinforcement needs to stop before the pivot point, or it becomes a lever. It will eventually tear next to the reinforcement.”
Silence.
I wrote as fast as I could, my mind already mapping the fix.
Stop the tape at the pivot. Shift the ease. Strengthen without bulk.
Preserve the silhouette. 
Mara drew the curtain back.
Charlie stepped out, still in the dress, one finger on the spot indicating strain. Mara grabbed chalk and marked a line on the garment.
I watched his face: controlled, determined.
[clearly] “And this is why,” Mara said to me, curt, “we don’t test with people who only wear.”
Her eyes flicked to him.
[clearly] “Here on out, we test with Charlie." 
Charlie’s ears went slightly pink. I kept my voice flat. 
“And we log everything,” I said, already flipping to a clean page in my notebook. “Every deviation from the original design. Every reinforcement. Every reason.”
Mara nodded. [clearly] “Exactly. Make it defensible.” She turned to Charlie.
[clearly] “You can take it off now,” she said. “Then, write me a report.”
Charlie blinked. [meekly] “A report.”
[clearly] “Yes,” Mara said. “Details. Where it pulled. What caused it. What you propose. In plain language. No poetry.”
“Yes Mara.”


He disappeared behind the curtain.
Mara looked at my notebook.
“Title it,” she said.
I wrote at the top of the page, in neat block letters:
**DESIGN REALISATION — PROTOTYPE 1 — STRESS TEST LOG**
Then underneath, because it mattered, because it named what we were building:
**Tester:** *Charlie Rossignol; **Purpose:** *durability & mobility without silhouette compromise*; **Notes:** *equipment, not theatre*

Mara’s gaze flicked over my shoulder, and her mouth twitched again.
[clearly] “Now we do this properly.”
The second prototype was a jacket. Different cut, different sleeve head, but the same intention: make it survive the day without turning it into armour. Mara hung it on the mannequin and stood back, chin lifted. Lucy and I watched her tug on the sleeves.
She turned to us.
[clearly] “So, this time,” she said to Lucy, “we’re not sending *you* out to crash test it.”
Lucy half-grinned as she blithely took a step back.
[gaily] “Bless,” she said with a flick of the hand. “Thank you.”
Mara ignored her.
[clearly] “Charlie.”
Charlie looked up from the bench where he’d been hand-stitching a reinforcement tape onto a waistband. He put his needle down carefully, precisely, and stood. Mara held up the jacket with two fingers.
[clearly] “Same drill.”
[meekly] “Right. Where’s the log sheet?”
The unease hadn’t vanished: it had been translated into structure, into process. He wordlessly took the sheet from her and moved toward the fitting area.
Mara snapped the curtain closed with a decisive tug.
With the third prototype, Mara made no announcement, but simply held it up: a skirt, new cut, new waistband, a clever closure arrangement we’d borrowed from an extant garment plate. 
She looked around the room.
[clearly] “Charlie.”
Charlie set his work down and stood.
“Yes Mara.” Then, he quietly added, [meekly] “Could— we make sure the curtain rail is fixed. It catches.”
Mara’s eyes flicked to the curtain hardware as if it had personally offended her.
[clearly] “Yes,” she said, “we'll get it fixed.”
By the fourth garment, no one had named it — but everyone was using it. It had become routine. 
A new jacket had been pinned, a clever cut I’d been excited about. It looked perfect on the mannequin. The silhouette was right. The closures sat where they should. 
Lucy ran her fingers over the seams.
"Looks just about ready to me."
Mara didn’t even look up from her pattern paper.
[clearly] “Not yet.”
Charlie, at the bench, lifted his head.
[meekly] “I can do it now, if you want me to.”
[clearly] “Do it,” Mara said. “Ten minutes. Break it on purpose.”
Charlie lifted the clipboard with the log sheet from its hook, removed the jacket from the mannequin and took both to the fitting area.
The curtain closed with a crisp snick.
