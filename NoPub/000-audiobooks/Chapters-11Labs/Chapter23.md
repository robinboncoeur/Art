# Chapter 23

## Celeste

I stopped.
*Something's not right.*
Charlie stared at the bottle, but didn't move.
I had a look at the label.
*Spironolactone.*
That told me nothing.
[clearly] "What is this, Charlie?"
His shoulders rose slightly. He swallowed.
[timidly] "Medicine."
[clearly] "No shit." I pressed my lips together briefly, exhaled sharply through my nose. "What's it for?"
His eyes had gone vacant, staring at nothing.
[clearly] "Charlie?"
He shrugged slightly.
[timidly] "It's to— *stop* things."
I closed my eyes for an instant and gave my head a tiny shake.
[puzzled] "Things?"
He nodded. The colour had returned to his face.
[timidly] "Things that were, like, happening to me."
My annoyance vanished.
[worried] "What was happening to you, Charlie?"
[timidly] "My legs."
All sorts of conditions affecting lower limbs chased each other through my head.
[worried] "Oh my lands, what was happening to your legs?"
Charlie's eyes were fixed on my feet.
I could barely hear the word.
[timidly] "Hair," he murmured.
I felt my jaw click.
[irritated] "What?"
His eyes slowly rose to meet mine.
[softly] "Hair was starting to grow on my legs."
I sat down — hard — in the other chair. A crow outside the window offered his opinion. Another joined it.
[emphatic] "Hair," I said finally.
He nodded.
I looked at the bottle again.
Then at Charlie.
[clearly] “You are taking medication to stop hair growing on your legs.”
Another nod.
I put the bottle carefully on the table.
Pulled the towel tighter.
[clearly] “Why?”
I looked at his face then.
Anguish. 
His jaw quivered slightly even as his shoulders rose more.
[timidly] "It was happening. I wanted it to stop."
[clearly] "You didn't want hair—"
[timidly] "No, not just that," he cut in. "All of it. All of—"
He stopped.
And stared at the floor.
The beating of wings outside the windows told me the crows had heard enough.
I leaned back in my chair.
"Okay."
Charlie sat frozen in his chair.
I got up.
[clearly] "I'm going to get dressed. I'm ready to listen whenever you're ready to talk to me, Charlie."
I left the bottle on the table and went back to my room.
The ride to work was quiet.
A few evenings later, I was sitting alone on the couch. The TV was on, but I had no idea what was happening on the screen. I yawned. 
[soft] *Might make an early mark.*
And then, Charlie appeared.
[timidly] "Celeste?"
He approached me — cautiously, I thought. Sat down slowly on the other end of the couch.
I waited.
[timidly] "You said you, um, would listen," he said after a moment.
He wrapped his thin arms around himself, and tipped his head to one side. 
[timidly] "Not sure how to explain this," he started.
"Begin anywhere."
He took in a breath.
[timidly] "So, the reason I couldn't tell you more the other day — you know, when you *found* the medication was, well, I didn't really know how to explain it."
His face scrunched with frustration.
[timidly] "I mean, I never really thought it through." He looked down. "I just knew I had to." 
My brows rose a fraction. 
[urgently and softly] "Celeste, I was freaking out." He squeezed his eyes shut for a moment. "My body was starting to change and it was freaking me out."
My brows rose a bit more.
[timidly] "At first, I thought it was Wardrobe. Like— if I started being like Graham, everyone there would start thinking of me differently. I wouldn't be cool there anymore. You know."
I suppressed a laugh.
[clearly] "*You*, be like Graham. [snort] Yeah, like that would happen."
[timidly] "You know what I mean."
My mouth went sideways.
[clearly] "No, actually I don't." I squinted at him. "I thought maintenance proved it."
[timidly] "No, I mean—" He puffed. "I mean, like a *bloke*."
[clearly] "But Charlie, you—"
I stopped. 
He'd gone still. 
The look in his eyes froze the thought before it could come out.
Outside, a curlew announced curfew, her shrieks echoing through the night stillness.
I permitted myself a wry smile.
"Sorry," I said. "I was talking, not listening. Please go on."
In his eyes I saw that anguish again.
I waited.
Then his shoulders lowered, barely.
[timidly] “I just—”
He looked away again.
[timidly] "I just couldn't be like— that."
I nodded.
"Fine. You don't want to be like Graham."
He dropped his gaze.
[timidly] "Or, like my dad."
I lifted my head and caught him glancing at me.
[soft] *Oh.*
"So, just— generally like a bloke."
He dipped his head to one side, eyes on his twisting fingers. He stole another glance at me.
[annoyed] "What?"
[timidly] "Nothing."
[annoyed] "Bullshit. You keep looking at me."
His ears went red. His mouth did that Charlie-pout.
[timidly] "I guess I'm, um, sort-of worried."
"Worried?"
He not so much nodded as twitched.
[timidly] "That you might— hate me."
I looked at him: proper, steady.
I didn't really know what to say to that.
[soft] *I didn't hate him, obviously. But what do you say to that?*
"Well, I don't." I got up off the couch. Stretched. "Going to bed, Charlie. See you in the morning."
I could feel his eyes on me as I went to my room.
