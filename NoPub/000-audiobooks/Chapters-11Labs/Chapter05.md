# Chapter 5

## Text

[Quiet, reflective narration] Mara didn’t test people the way teachers do. Teachers would announce a test, watch you sweat, and then moralise the result. Mara tested you the way you tested a seam: under her fingers, quietly, looking for strain.
Charlie was back for a second morning. Same early arrival. Same old leather bag. Same clean hands. Same careful stillness. 
Mara pointed at a tin on the table.
[clearly] “Needles,” she said. “Sort them. Then you’re on the mending pile.”
Charlie poured the needles out onto a cloth. With a kind of tidy focus that made the task look dignified, he began arranging them by size.
Mara’s next test came ten minutes later, when she “accidentally” left a pair of vintage shears too close to the edge of the table. 
[Quiet, reflective narration] Charlie noticed the shears. His eyes flicked to them, then to Mara. He didn’t touch them but nudged the cloth closer, stabilised the table edge with his palm, and slid them back with two fingers — careful, respectful — like he was returning a bird to its perch.
Mara didn’t look up. 
[Quiet, reflective narration] At first I didn't think she'd noticed at all. But presently, I saw the smallest change in her mouth. 
She knew. 
Of course she knew.
[Quiet, reflective narration] Mara sent him to the mending pile: a basket of small catastrophes — popped seams, torn cuffs, fraying apron ties. The difficult ones, the ones where haste would show. Charlie studied each item the way he’d studied the torn dress yesterday: calm, quiet. He chose thread that colour-matched with an uncanny instinct. He stitched with even tension, no puckering or desperate pulling.
Charlie’s attention to Mara was constant and respectful.
[Warm, intimate narration] Charlie’s attention to me was — different.
[Warm, intimate narration] It was as if his eyes kept finding me on their own and each time they did, he would look away, as if noticing me was an invasion of privacy.
[Warm, intimate narration] Was it a crush?

From the rail, I watched his hands gently — almost tenderly — coax thread and fabric to be friends.
“You’re stitching like you’ve done this for years,” I said.
Charlie’s hands paused for half a second, needle hovering. Then he kept going.
[quietly] “My mum,” he said quietly. “She made sewing fun. It was her game — she gave me harder and harder things to sew. The goal was for the repair to be invisible. If you make it invisible, it looks neat.”
[witty] “That’s true,” I replied. “And if you make it neat here, Mara doesn’t kill you.”
His mouth twitched into a fragile smile, which quickly vanished. His eyes flicked from my face to his work like the paws of a cat picking its way across a hot path.
[gently] “Thanks,” he said after a moment.
“For what?”
[gently] “For yesterday.” He looked down at his hands. “For— for getting me in.”
I shook my head.
[quietly] No.
[quietly] I don’t want gratitude*
[clearly] “*You* got you in,” I said. “You turned up. You worked.”
His shoulders loosened slightly. He threaded another needle, hands steady.
[gently] “I just—"
He stopped, so I did my usual: I decided what he was trying to say.
[clearly] “You mean you don’t want to make me uncomfortable.”
His hazel eyes widened, startled. Then his face fell again with a nod as quick as shame.
[softly] “Yes,” he whispered.
[clearly] “Fine. Let's keep it that way.”
The steamer chose just then to emit a hiss. We both turned to look at it.
[gently] “It’s just—" I heard him stammer. "You’re— you’re a lot.”
I frowned.
[clearly] “A lot?”
[gently] “I mean, you’re—" He paused to put in a final stitch and snipped the thread short. Finally: "You make things make sense.”
I let his words sit between us.
[clearly] “Right then,” I said, picking up a bodice with a frayed edge tear. "Let's see if you can make this tear disappear.”
He studied it and his face smoothed. Almost smiled.
[gently] “Okay.”
I turned to go back to the rail.
[gently] “I feel I should tell you— and Mara,“ I heard his voice behind me, the words spilling out, rushed. ”Maintenance was just a summer job. It seems like people are teaching me things— trusting me with things. It wouldn’t be fair if I didn't tell you.”
I paused, hand resting on a garment bag.
“Tell me what?”


His eyes were fixed on the seam.
[gently] “I’m meant to be starting an apprenticeship next year. Welding. At the shipyard.” He swallowed. “Dad’s lined it up.”
That landed like a duck expecting water but finding ice.
I looked at his hands. 
His fingers around the needle. 
His arms. 
His long hair tied back with an elastic.
[softly] Welder.
[softly] Make it make sense.
[clearly] “Did he line up maintenance for you as well?” I asked.
He shrugged, small. “Yeah."
I left the rail. Mara gave me a look but I ignored it.
"Is welding something that interests you?"
Charlie frowned slightly.
[gently] "Dad says it's a good trade."
[clearly] "That's not what I asked."
He opened his mouth.
Closed it again and rubbed his forehead.
Silence stretched. The steamer hissed somewhere behind us.
[gently] “I just—" he began, then stopped. His voice dropped. “It's solid work.”
*There it was.*
*Survival.*
I placed my finger lightly on the fabric near his seam.
[clearly] “Is this solid work?”
He shook his head with a faint puff. “This is— dresses.”
Across the room, Mara’s voice cut through the hiss of the steamer.
[clearly] “Rossignol!”
Charlie’s head snapped up. [gently] “Yes Mara?”
Mara held up a sleeve with a tare near the cuff. [clearly] “This one. Show everyone how you do it.”
Charlie went very still.
[gently] “Yes, Mara.”
He rose and took the sleeve from her.
