# Chapter 11

## Celeste

Mara put a ledger on the table one morning.
Thick. Hard cover. The kind that you'd worry about offending if you dropped it. It claimed a spot between pincushion and shears on the repairs table, beside the tin of chalk, accompanied by a wooden stool that hadn't seen varnish in decades.
On the first page of the ledger Mara had already ruled headings in her angular hand.
GARMENT, DATE ISSUED, ISSUED TO, PROBLEM REPORTED, REPAIR, RE-TEST
Mara tapped the headings once.
[clearly] "The headings are not set in stone," she said. "This is a starting point."
I looked up.
"This is for cataloguing repairs?"
She shook her head.
[clearly] "Not just repairs. *Everything*. No more mending pile filled with guesses and assumptions."
I glanced at the repairs table, where all items to be mended once lived. I had watched Charlie finish the last item the night before, so I was surprised to see a folded up chemise sitting next to the sewing machine, a bit lonely.
Mara noticed it too. Her lips tightened.
[sharply] "Whose is that?"
Charlie appeared just then, quietly. He'd set his leather bag on the floor and had started undoing the buttons of his jacket when he followed Mara's gaze.
[gently] "Oh, that? One of the actresses dropped it off last night, just at closing. You had just—"
[sharply] "Who?"
Charlie startled slightly.
[gently] "Lily."
He removed his denim jacket.
Mara picked up a spiral notebook and fixed her gaze on his face.
[clearly] "Your first task this morning is to get details on how that garment failed. From Lily."
[gently] "But—" He threw a puzzled look at the window.
[clearly] "She's an actress in the palace."
His eyes went a bit wider as she handed him the spiral notebook and the chemise.
[gently] "Um, I *did* have a look at it last night," he said. "I could probably tell—"
[clearly] "'Probably' isn't data," Mara said quietly.
His face fell. Mara saw it.
Of course she did.
[sharply] "Is there a problem?"
[gently] "No-no," he said hastily. "I just thought—"
[sharply] "That your testing was going to answer every question?" 
She waited. 
He bit his lip, his hands shaking slightly as he slipped his jacket back on and picked a pen out of the tin.
[clearly] "Details," Mara said. "We need to know what she was doing when it failed, if it was pinching, if it was something she'd noticed was starting to fail, and any other information you can think of."
[gently] "Yes, Mara."
He glanced at me as he moved to the door.
The door clicked shut. 
Mara's eyes met mine.
I went back to work.
Later that morning, I opened the window. A cold wind had come up and was blowing leaves around the carpark. I propped the window partly open to let in a bit of fresh air, idly wondering why I couldn't smell the usual hot chips porta-pottie medley.
I had just returned to doing invoices when the door opened.
Lucy came in, wearing a huge grin. She sidled up to me.
[happily] "Who sent Charlie out to interview the wolves?"
I tipped my chin at Mara.
[happily] "He's out there like a reporter at a hostage scene," Lucy said, lowering her voice. "Asking Lily a bazillion questions and scribbling away—"
I nodded.
"He takes his job seriously."
Lucy gave a sharp laugh.
[happily] "Lily never knew what hit her." She tossed her head. "And then, this other actress—"
The door slowly opened and Charlie stepped into the workroom. He was studying his notes, his mouth to one side.
Mara looked up from her work.
[clearly] "Did you get it all?"
Charlie tipped his head slightly to one side.
[gently] "I think so."
Mara's eyes rested on the notebook.
[clearly] "Have a look?"
His neck reddened slightly as he passed it over to her. She glanced over it, and handed it back.
[clearly] "Can you read this?"
[gently] "Mostly yes."
[clearly] "Transcribe it."
She pointed at the ledger.

Over the next few days, the weather got colder. The number of actresses visiting Wardrobe seemed to increase. Mara finally didn't even look up from her work, but would simply point at Charlie.
The furious notebook scribbling stopped: Charlie wrote directly into the ledger.
He had just closed it one afternoon and had picked up a jacket to repair when the door opened and a blond actress strode in carrying a linen chemise. She stood before Mara, hands on hips, ignoring her gesture towards the mending corner.
Mara looked up then.
[clearly] "Repair?"
"Yes."
Mara nodded at the other end of the room.
[clearly] "Charlie is responsible for repairs. He will need details on how the failure happened."
[sharply] "I don't like discussing intimate garments with a bloke."
I could see Charlie stiffen.
Mara's eyes sharpened.
[clearly] “You don’t have to like it. His job doesn’t change.”
The blonde straightened.
[sharply] "I don't think it's inappropriate."
Mara stood. 
[smoothly] "You have a right to your opinion," she said silkily. "The more information we have on what went wrong, the better able we are to prevent problems in the future. Whether you assist us in that is up to you."
The blonde dropped the linen garment on the worktable and turned on her heels. Mara wordlessly picked up the chemise, folded it and inserted it at the bottom of the repair pile, which had accumulated that afternoon.
"Whenever you can get to it," she said to Charlie.
He nodded.
Friday afternoons became design review sessions. Mara, Charlie and I would sit at her worktable, ledger open — I would take notes. 
Charlie's notes had translated into garment failure patterns.
Charlie usually had the floor.
Mara would say very little.
I said nothing.
We were halfway through one of these sessions that Lucy came in with the blond actress, a jacket folded over her arm. Lucy went straight to Charlie.
[happily] "I hope we're not interrupting something—"
"Carry on," said Mara.
Lucy put the jacket on the table.
[happily] "So, this isn't actually my jacket," she began when Mara interrupted her.
[sharply] "Whose is it?"
Lucy startled.
[surprised] "Um, well, Bree's, actually."
[clearly] "Then Bree needs to bring it in."
Lucy turned the jacket over. "It's just where the sleeve's come loose—"
"Perhaps," Mara said. "We still need to know *why*. And *how*."
[annoyed] "But why is that so important?" the blonde interjected. "We never did all that before."
Mara turned to Charlie.
[clearly] "Show her."
Charlie swallowed, then opened the ledger.
[gently] "We're looking for failure patterns," he said quietly, not looking at anyone, "to prevent those failures in future designs."
The steamer hissed. A diesel truck rumbled by the partly open window.
"I'll ask Bree to pop 'round," Lucy said finally.
After Lucy and the actress left, Charlie glanced up. Mara tipped her chin at him.
[clearly] "Carry on."
We had been back at work for only a few minutes — me at the rails, Charlie back at the repairs table — when the door opened briskly and a tall actress with an imposing bearing entered Wardrobe. She glanced around the room. When she found Charlie, she strode up to his table.
[loudly] "Hi, I'm Bree," she said in a voice designed to carry to the budget seats.
Charlie wordlessly pulled the jacket from the pile and opened the ledger.
[gently] "May I ask what happened, please."
[loudly] "Of course!"
His smile was doing its best, but his eyes said overwhelmed. Bree was at least a foot taller and held herself like royalty. I could picture her doing King Lear in the title role.
[gently] "Would you like to sit down, Bree?" he offered.
[loudly] "Thought you'd never ask." She dropped herself unceremoniously into the proffered chair. I could hear it creak. "So, what do you want to know?"
Charlie consulted the ledger.
[gently] "What were you doing at the time?"
[loudly] "I was thrusting with a sword."
Charlie blinked.
[gently] "I see. Could you show—"
[Building tension, measured pace] Before he could finish, her fist flew at him, stopping inches away from his startled face. She burst into gales of laughter.
[laughter] "Oh, sorry sugar, I didn't mean to—" She glanced at Mara, biting her lower lip. 
[apologetic] "I— I really didn't mean to—"
Mara said nothing but went back to work.
Charlie's mouth curved valiantly. 
Bree's smile quickly regained most of its cheerfulness.
[loudly] "Sorry, hon," she said. "I tend to lose myself in the moment." She wrapped her arms around herself. "What else can I tell you?"
Charlie carefully examined the sleeve.
[gently] "Did the jacket feel tight in the shoulders?"
Bree shook her head.

-

[gently] "Did it suddenly let go, or did you notice it loosening a bit at first?"
Bree's eyebrows rose.
[loudly] "I didn't notice. Sorry."
Charlie gave a quick nod, and turned the jacket inside out.
[gently] "I think I have what I need," he said. "I might reinforce both sleeves when I do the repair so it doesn't happen again. Are there any other issues with this jacket?"
Bree shook her head and rose. 
[loudly] "You're the talk of the Faire," she said to Charlie, who gaped at her. "Keep up the good work!"
A couple of days later, Lily showed up at around two in the afternoon with a dour-faced actress holding a bodice. Lily was carrying a bag from the Faire bakery.
[happily] "Afternoon tea for my favourite people in the world," she announced cheerfully.
"Lovely. Thank you," Mara said, indicating the tearoom. The dour-faced woman found Charlie at a sewing machine.
[sarcasm] "Do you even know how to use that thing?" she asked.
To my surprise, he looked unfazed.
[gently] "Yes, I do," he replied. "How can I help you?"
He calmly took down details about the bodice — when, where, how — and then said:
[gently] "We can add a bit of reinforcing if you'd like. So it doesn't split again."
[happily] "That would be lovely," Lily interjected before dour-face could speak. "You do such lovely work, Charlie." [happily] The scowl on the other woman's face had eased a bit. "I'll be back to the palace in a tick, Vanessa," Lily continued.
Vanessa mumbled something, approximated a smile, then turned and left.
[confiding] "Don't mind her," Lily said gently to Charlie. “She's going through a nasty breakup."
Charlie nodded, his face one of expectation.
[gently] "Was there—" he began, but Lily giggled and shook her head.
[happily] "Well, no." She paused. 
[confiding] "Well, actually, yes." She produced a pair of black leggings from her bag. "I was wondering—"
Charlie stared at the leggings.
Mara appeared behind him. She spoke clearly, without emotion.
[clearly] "We don't do alterations or repairs on personal garments."
Lily's shoulders went up. She quickly picked the leggings from Charlie's hands. 
[apologetic] "Oh, sorry," she murmured. "It's just— it's so cold in the palace, especially in the early mornings. But I'm sorry, I didn't mean to—"
[clearly] "The chemise and extra petticoats don't keep you warm?"
Lily shook her head, then got up to go. Mara held out her hand.
[clearly] "Have a look?"
She carefully inspected the leggings: they were of a heavy stretch cotton with a soft inner layer. The gusset in the crotch had given way. She handed the leggings to Charlie.
[clearly] "This is a common fail point for leggings. Any ideas on how to fix it?"
His brows narrowed as he rubbed the stretch cotton between his fingers.
[gently] "We would need this type of material," he said slowly. "I'd replace the gusset completely, and then, add a reinforcement layer on the inside."
Mara turned to Lily.
"We will fix it."
She turned to go back to her worktable. Before she sat down, she said to me,
[clearly] "Time to rethink the winter chemise. And possibly, the petticoat."
Less than a week later, a new chemise design was spread out on Mara's worktable. I was on the phone to Lauren's linen supplier when Lily appeared. She went straight to Charlie.
[happily] "Celeste said you have the material."
Charlie slowly put down his work, carefully arranging everything so he could quickly pick it all back up where he'd left off.
"Came in yesterday." He reached into the repair basket and produced a bag. "If you can wait, I can do the repair straight away."
[happily] "Oh, that would be amazing!" She handed him the leggings almost reluctantly. "They're my favourite pair," she said, her eyes fixed on the garment.
"I'll be careful."
[enthusiastic] "Oh, I'm sure you will be!" she breathed. "Your hands are so gentle. They remind me of Bree's."
Her face reddened suddenly.
Charlie gave her a crooked smile, then slowly started unpicking the gusset. She watched him work, the side of her lower lip gripped between her teeth. Every now and then, he would glance at her with a quick smile, then return to his work. When the old piece finally came free, he laid it over the new cotton and began marking around it.
[happily] “You're replacing the whole thing?”
“Yes. It failed.”
[happily] Lily gave a quick nod. “Right.”
When they got to the overlocker Lily glanced at the tearoom.
"Oh dear. Your coffee has gone cold."
He shrugged.
"It was nice of you to bring afternoon tea."
After Lily left, Mara pointed at the ledger.
[clearly] "Just a quick note: *what* you repaired, and *why*."
Charlie turned his head.
"Because the gusset was coming apart?"
[clearly] "No. Because chemise and petticoats do not always keep an actress warm."
Charlie opened the ledger.

That evening, Mara had just turned off the steamer when the door opened. Bree sauntered in followed by the tall blonde, who cast a suspicious eye at Charlie. Bree, however, wrapped her arm around Charlie's shoulders. His eyes went saucer-shaped.
[loudly] "I just wanted to say thank you for fixing Lily's leggings," she said cheerfully. "Those mornings in the palace are getting colder all the time, even with the fireplace."
Charlie curved his lips upwards towards eyes that didn't quite agree.
[gently] "Your jacket should be ready tomorrow. Anything else?"
Bree chuckled.
[loudly] "No, hun, just wanted to say thanks. Oh, and Sarah's chemise," she said, indicating the pinch-faced blonde beside her. "Any idea when it might be ready?"
Charlie swallowed and glanced at Mara, who had pulled the chemise from the bottom of the pile.
[clearly] "Care to tell us what the issue is?"
Charlie grabbed his notebook and pen, and perched attentively on the wooden stool.
Sarah huffed.
[cold] "Really?"
Mara didn't blink.
[clearly] "It would help. Charlie hasn't found any signs of strain or tearing."
Sarah's scowl deepened. She glanced at Charlie's face, and puffed again.
[cold] "It— binds."
Mara waited. Sarah's scowl deepened.
[cold] "Across the bust," she said finally.
Charlie's head was in the ledger when he asked:
[gently] "Would the next size up be more comfortable?"
Silence.
He looked up then and startled. Sarah was glaring at him.
[loudly] "Oi! Girlfriend! Relax." Bree's voice bounced off the wall. 
Sarah's demeanour didn't change.
[angrily] "Are you saying I'm—"
[smoothly] "Charlie is asking if the next size up would be more comfortable," Mara said evenly. "We can then make it more flattering if you want. A chemise should not bind."
Sarah's fists slowly unclenched.
[cold] "Sorry, I'm just not used to some strange male—"
Her body swayed suddenly as Bree gave her a shove.
[loudly] "C'mon, Sarah! Charlie's one of us," Bree boomed. "Give it a rest, will ya?"
Sarah shrugged, her face still sullen. Mara studied the chemise for a moment, then handed it to Charlie.
[clearly] "One size up?"
Charlie nodded.
[gently] "And darts," he added gently. His eyes flicked at Sarah, then back to the chemise. 
Bree sidled up to him.
[loudly] "Show me what you would do, Charlie."
He gently smoothed the garment on its side on the table.
[gently] "Just here, Bree." His fingers moved respectfully over the material. "It would give the chemise a nice silhouette, but without binding or bunching."
I noticed Sarah studying him, watching his hands, his eyes. 
Her face slowly lost some of its hardness.
Two days later, Bree collected her jacket and Sarah's chemise.
[enthusiastic] "Oh, that feels so much better," Bree exclaimed, pulling the jacket on over her hoodie. "You're a magician, Charlie!"
Charlie's smile went a bit crooked as Bree thrust her arm forward, away from him this time. I could see he was eyeing seams nervously.
They held.
[emphatic] "Now, if only you could work your magic on Lily," Bree said.
Charlie's mouth fell open.
[gently] "Did the leggings fail?"
Bree chortled.
[enthusiastic] "No, silly, not her leggings. Her *heart*."
Charlie sat still for a moment, a momentary frown came and went.
[gently curious] "What do you mean? She really likes you."
At first, Bree's eyes popped wide in surprise. She gave her head a disbelieving shake.
[emphatic] "Nah!"
Charlie frowned.
[gently serious] "Yes," he said seriously. She does!" He flinched as Bree slapped him briskly on the shoulder.
[emphatic] "You better be right," she said as she left.
The door slammed.
Charlie flicked a pinched look at me. I suppressed a grin and went over to where Mara was pinning the new chemise on the mannequin. 
The material was a heavier, yet softer linen.
And the ledger sat confidently between pincushion and shears, vindicated.
