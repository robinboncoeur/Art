# Chapter 13

## Celeste

Mara called me to the cutting table.
[clearly] “Bring the ledger.”
Lauren was already there, sleeves rolled up, pencil behind her ear like it had grown there. In front of her: printed emails, order confirmations, a delivery docket stamped in red. On top sat a single sheet covered in neat columns.
Sarah sat on a stool with her arms folded.
Tahlia listened in from her overlocker.
Charlie sat on the unvarnished chair.
Mara tapped the stack of papers.
[clearly] “They're hiring for the new marketplace on the other side of the Faire,” she said. “They want more units.”
So that's what all that diesel traffic was about. We'd heard something about how things were expanding into the forest, but I'd figured it was additional picnic tables.
"How many costumes do they want?" Sarah asked.
Lauren slid the top sheet toward her.
Sarah's jaw dropped.
[shocked] “Thirty-six?”
[briskly] “That's just this batch," Lauren said. "With a follow-on option if they hire more people.”
“Thirty-six!”
[briskly] “And they want delivery dates,” Lauren added. “Not just ‘when it’s ready.’”
I heard a low whistle come from the overlocker.
[wonder] “Thirty-six,” Tahlia said. “That’s not— boutique.”
[clearly] “No,” Mara said. “That’s work.”
She looked at me, and the room shifted into a structure.
I opened the ledger. The pages tried to reassure me it could work: headings, repeated fields, signatures. Charlie’s handwriting, increasingly steady. Mara’s marginal corrections. The blunt, unwavering language of process. Mara pointed to the most recent entries.
[briskly] “How many prototypes did we run last week?”
Charlie answered before I could.
“Eleven,” he blurted, then glanced at me, eyes widening. [meekly] “Oh— sorry.“
Mara tipped her chin at him.
[clearly] "Carry on."
I nodded at him, brows raised. He swallowed.
“Um— across three body types. One full redo on the ‘well-nourished’ block." He took in a breath. "Two seam-finish changes. And— the underarm guard adjustment.”
Mara pointed at Lauren’s sheet.
“And how many *finished* garments left the building?”
Lauren’s pencil tapped the paper once. “Nine.”
Sarah's brow furrowed. “Only nine?”
Lauren turned her head slightly toward her.
[briskly] “Nine *finished* garments,” she said, “that's nine more than most people manage without a system.”
She slid another, uglier sheet forward.
[briskly] “Mara asked me to tally costs,” Lauren said. “This lists *everything*, including the things you forget to count when you’re focused on making things pretty.”
Mara’s mouth tightened.
[briskly] “We’re profitable on small runs,” Lauren continued. “We’re— interesting on larger ones. But we're bleeding time.”
We stared at the sheets.
[muttering] “If we try to do thirty-six outfits without changing anything," Sarah muttered, "we’ll—"
[briskly] “Be exhausted beyond repair,” Lauren finished for her.
Sarah snorted. [snort] “Not realistic.”
Mara’s eyes flicked to her.
[sarcasm] “Just sayin',” Sarah said. "The maths don't math."
“We could— standardise more steps,” Charlie said slowly, “Use templates. Make hardware packs. Cutting order.”
I nodded.
“That’s the shape of it.”
Mara's brows rose slightly.
[clearly] “Celeste. You can see it.”
I *could* see it. I could feel my mind doing its favourite thing: taking chaos and converting it into something usable, repeatable.
Lauren was watching my face.
“What is it you see?”
“We need *operations*,” I said. “Not vibes or heroics. Operations.”
Sarah shrugged. [sarcastic] “Operations.”
“Yes,” I said. “Inventory. Vendor schedules. Production planning. Quality Assurance that doesn’t depend on Mara being in three places at once.”
Mara cleared her throat.
[clearly] "There's something else to think about. Staffing. Hours." She turned to me. “And you’re going back to school.”
I stared at her. I hadn't told Mara.
Heat climbed into my face.
“I am,” I said. “Uni. MBA. Or at least the pathway to it.”
Sarah lifted a brow. [surprised] “You? Business?”
“Yes.”


Mara didn’t let Sarah’s surprise take oxygen.
[clearly] “Wardrobe needs a manager who understands the environment. It won’t do well under an outsider,” Mara said. “It needs someone who understands how to make it survive growth.” She tapped the ledger. “You understand our standards. You understand our policies. And you’re already thinking in systems.”
Then Mara’s tone went flat.
[flatly] “One problem: we don’t have the money to send you.”

[softly] Send me? That was never on my radar.

[quietly] “I've been saving up,” I said quietly. “And I'll continue to work. I'll' rent out my spare room, too.”
Mara's face went impassive.
[clearly] "Even operations won't prevent burnout." 
Lauren was nodding.
[briskly] “We need to do this the Wardrobe way,” she said.
"But—"
[briskly] "Celeste, listen. We're not doing charity. This is investment."
She tapped the papers and glanced at me. 
[briskly] “We set up a fund. Transparent. Written. Agreed.” She tapped her finger on the table. "This won’t happen on good intentions and hope.”
Mara held Lauren’s gaze for a long second.
[clearly] “Let's write it,” she said finally.
Lauren handed me her pen. 
[cheerfully] “Provide all the details you can about Uni,” she said, warm again. “We need numbers. Fees. Timeline. Units.”
Sarah folded her arms.
[sarcastic] "So, what do we do about staffing, then?"
Lauren turned to her.
"Could you go full-time?"
[sarcastic] "Of course!" Sarah said. "But I'm just one person—"
Lauren began counting on her fingers.
"Tahlia's full-time, Leah's full-time." She thoughtfully threw out a third finger. "Perhaps I could cut back a bit at Home-Health—"
Mara shook her head. "You shouldn't need to do that.”
[meekly] "*I* could stay later," Charlie broke in.
[clearly] "No, you can't," Mara said. "You have a bus to catch."
Sarah drew her head back and frowned at him.
"Where do you live?"
[meekly] "North Geelong."
"Actually, we're closer to Lara," Lauren corrected. "He has to change buses at Torquay. Makes for such a long bus ride."
[meekly] "I don't mind, Mum," he muttered.
"So, we might need to hire more staff," Sarah said.
I could see Mara tense. She gave Sarah one of her looks.
[calmly] "It's not that easy, Sarah," Lauren said. Her voice was gentle. "You need the right people. Not everyone's a quick read."
[clearly] "Look, we're taking the order," Mara said. "Celeste, work out the details. We'll reconvene when you know more."

By the end of the week, I'd not only sorted — at least, on paper — how this was all going to be possible, but I'd submitted my application for the Master's programme, and even found someone to rent a room to.
A girl with a German accent — one of the Faire palace actresses who worked with Lily — moved in that weekend.
