# Chapter 7

## Text

[Quiet, reflective] I was at the rail tagging garment bags when Lauren Rossignol arrived.
[Quiet, reflective] She didn't come to Wardrobe like a mother arriving at a principal's office. Instead, she stepped into the room respectfully, like someone realising she'd entered a place where skilled hands were at work. A canvas tote on her shoulder and her car keys in her hand, her gaze swept the room, at the rails, the mannequin wearing Lily's palace dress with pins in place.
Her hair was pulled back too tightly for vanity.
Her lipstick was absent.
Her expression was not.
Mara was at the cutting table, pencil behind her ear, studying the pattern paper spread before her.
She looked up.
[clearly] "Can I help you?"
Lauren paused. Her gaze had settled on the mending table for a few seconds longer than necessary, where a figure sat with their back to her, absorbed in a garment. She turned to Mara.
[flat] "I'm Charles' mother," she said finally.
Mara didn't move, but something in her eyes adjusted, like a lens clicking into focus.
"I see," Mara said.
Lauren frowned slightly.
[clearly] "I expected him to be working in maintenance," she said. "He told me over dinner last night he was working here."
[calmly] "He is." Mara studied her calmly. "And you are wondering why."
Lauren bit her lip and nodded. 
Mara pointed at the chair next to her table.
[calmly] "Would you like to sit down?"
Lauren sat, carefully. She placed her keys on her knee instead of the table. Her tote stayed on her shoulder.
Mara eyed her, impassive.
[calmly] "My name is Mara." The words came out quiet, measured. "What would you like to know?"
Lauren was looking around the room again, searching. She hastily returned, as if remembering her manners.
[seriously] "Sorry. I'm Lauren." She stopped, pressing her fist against her lips for a beat. "I just want to understand why he's here and not in maintenance."
Mara's mouth went into a thin line.
[calmly] “Maintenance gave him work that needs shoulders. He needs work that requires hands.”
Lauren stared at her for a moment.
"His father arranged Maintenance."
Mara held Lauren in her gaze.
[calmly] "Yes," she said.
Lauren's shoulders moved slightly forward. She took in a slow, deep breath and let it out in a short puff.
And stared at the window.
Mara waited.
[seriously] "We're just concerned for his future,” Lauren said finally. She looked thoughtful for a moment, then seemed to remember something. Reaching into her tote bag, she produced a folded piece of cloth, which she handed to Mara.
[musing] "Charles showed me this last night," she said. "The lace had separated from the cap. He'd sewn it back."
Mara's lips twitched slightly. She slowly unfolded it.
They stared at it for a moment without speaking. Then, Mara's eyes rose to Lauren's.
[clearly] "He says you taught him."
Lauren's gaze dropped back to the cap.
"I did." She shrugged slightly. "It was— arts and crafts."
Mara's brows rose a fraction.
[gently] "Arts and crafts?"
Lauren's face softened slightly.
"You know, something to keep kids busy during the holidays."
Mara's look was piercing.
[forcefully] "No."
The word wasn't said so much as expelled. Lauren's eyes widened.
[clearly] "This isn't arts and crafts, Lauren." She turned the cap inside out, examining the stitching. "This is a foundation. This is how I would have done the job."
She pointed at the edge of the lace.
[clearly] "Most people just see lace that has come off." She tugged slightly at the seam. "A sewer looks for where it had let go." She put the cap on the table.
[clearly] "You didn't just teach sewing, but seeing."
Lauren's hand reached out and gently touched the lace. Her head bobbed in a barely perceptible nod. She glanced at Mara and then her eyes fell on the mannequin wearing the ornate gown. She took in the sewing machines on tables with folded fabric and bobbins of thread and tins for chalk, pins, and thimbles. 
And finally, stopped at the repair table.
Her eyes changed.
[gently] "Would you like to have a look around?" Mara said.
Lauren nodded again and pointed at the pattern on the table.
"May I ask what is this for?"
Mara gestured towards the open window. Morning at the Historical Faire was in full swing: the voices of actors in the marketplace proclaiming the virtues of their onions and turnips could be heard in the distance.
"New production. The Faire wants a shopkeeper line."
Lauren's eyebrows rose.



"You don't just do repairs, then?"
[clearly] "We did at first," Mara said, "when the Faire had just opened. But then, the owners weren't happy with what we were getting from overseas."
Lauren's head tilted as she studied the pattern.
"The costumes we made held up better," Mara continued. "And so, management kept ordering new ones to replace the others. There aren't that many of the overseas ones left."
The front-loader started its final spin rattle as the shouting of the vendors seemed to grow louder.
"So, this is— a trade."
[clearly] "Several trades," said Mara. "People think all we do is stitch torn seams." She stood. "We're a workshop. Cloth just happens to be our material."
Lauren looked up from the pattern. Her eyes went to the repair table again.
She glanced at me, and then, turned to Mara.
[gently] "So, what do I do?"
Mara's look didn't change.
"You don't do anything different."
"But Charles' father—" Lauren stopped.
Mara's look sharpened.
[clearly] "He will have his opinion."
Lauren looked at her.
[clearly] "He knows ships." Mara sat down. "You know Charlie. And his opinion doesn't change what you know."
Lauren grimaced.
"Still. Roger *is* Charles' father."
Mara put her arms on the table and leaned forward, looking straight at Lauren.
[gently] "No one is suggesting you ignore Roger's opinion." She held Lauren's gaze.
"Then what *are* you saying?"
[clearly] "I'm saying this: do not ignore your own."
Lauren looked away. 
I continued to quietly put tags on finished costumes. The metal hook of the hangers against the rails made a faint scraping sound as the costumes swayed.
And then, for a while, the only sounds were those of the Faire through the window and the faint hiss of the steamer.
I watched Lauren take in a breath and look around. 
Mara wordlessly watched her get up and walk over to Charlie.
He raised his eyes to meet hers, his head and his body completely still.
Her eyes moved from his face to his hands.
[gently] "What are you working on?"
He held up the seam.
And Lauren nodded.
