# Edits

[ *Celeste* ]

Mara put a ledger on the table one morning.

Thick. Hard cover. The kind that would you'd worry about offending if you dropped it. It claimed a spot between pincushion and shears, beside the tin of chalk, accompanied by a wooden stool that hadn't seen varnish in decades.

On the first page of the ledger Mara had already ruled headings in her angular hand.

GARMENT:  
DATE ISSUED:  
ISSUED TO:  
PROBLEM REPORTED:  
REPAIR:  
RE-TEST:

Mara tapped the headings once.

"The headings are not set in stone," she said. "This is a starting point."

I looked up.

"This is for cataloguing repairs?"

She shook her head.

"No. Everything. No more mending pile filled with guesses and assumptions."

I glanced over to the other end of the worktable, where the mending pile once lived. I had watched Charlie finish the last item the night before, so I was surprised to see a folded up chemise sitting on the edge, looking a bit abandoned.

Mara noticed it too. Her lips tightened.

"Who's is that?"

Charlie appeared just then, quietly. He started undoing the buttons of his jacket as he followed Mara's gaze.

"Oh, that? One of the actresses dropped it off last night, just at closing. You had just—"

"Who?"

Charlie startled slightly. He frowned, trying to remember.

"I think her name as Lily." He gave a nod. "Yeah, that was it."

He removed his denim jacket.

Mara picked up a spiral notebook. She fixed her gaze on his face.

"Your first task this morning is to get details on how that garment failed. From Lily."

"But—" He threw a puzzled look at the window.

"She's an actress in the marketplace."

His eyes went a bit wider as she handed him the spiral notebook and the chemise.

"Um, I *did* have a look at it last night," he said. "I could probably tell—"

"'Probably' isn't data," Mara said firmly.

His face fell. Mara saw it.

Of course she did.

"Is there a problem?"

"No-no," he said hastily. "I just thought—"

"That your testing abilities were going to answer every question?" 

She waited. 

He bit his lip, his hands shaking slightly as he slipped his jacket back on and picked a pen out of the tin.

"Details," Mara said. "We need to know what she was doing when it failed, if it was pinching, if it was something she'd noticed was starting to fail, and any other information you can think of."

"Yes, Mara."

He glanced at me as he moved to the door.

The door clicked shut. 

Mara's eyes met mine.

I went back to work.

Later that morning, I opened the window. A cold wind had come up and was blowing leaves around the carpark. I propped the window partly open to let in a bit of fresh air, wondering why I couldn't smell the usual hot chips porta-pottie medley.

I had just returned to invoices when the door opened.

Lucy came in, wearing a huge grin. She sidled up to me.

"Who sent Charlie out to interview the wolves?"

I tipped my chin at Mara.

"He's looking like a reporter for the local paper at a hostage scene," Lucy said, lowering  her voice. "Asked poor Lily a bazillion questions and scribbling away—"

I nodded.

"He takes his jobs seriously."

Lucy gave a sharp laugh.

"Lily never knew what hit her." She tossed her head. "And then, this other actress—"

The door burst open and Charlie tottered in, eyes scanning his notes. His mouth was to one side.

Mara looked up from her work.

"Did you get it all?"

Charlie tipped his head slightly to one side.

"I think so."

Mara's eyes rested on the notebook.

"Have a look?"

His neck reddened slightly as he passed it over to her. She glanced over it, and handed it back.

"Can you read this?"

"Yes."

"Good. Transcribe it."

She pointed at the ledger.

===

Over the next few days, the weather got colder. And the number of actresses bringing items to Wardrobe for repair increased. Mara wouldn't even look up from her work when one of them appeared but would simply point at Charlie.

Charlie's furious scribbling had settled: he wrote less into the notebook and, for simple repairs, entered details directly into the ledger.

He had just shut the ledger one afternoon and had picked up a jacket to repair when the door opened and a tall blond actress strode in carrying a linen chemise. She stood before Mara, hands on hips, ignoring her gesture towards the mending corner.

Mara looked up then.

"Repair?"

"Yes."

Mara nodded at the other end of the worktable.

"Charlie is responsible for repairs. He needs details on how the failure happened."

"I don't like discussing intimate garments with a bloke."

I could see Charlie stiffen.

Mara's eyes sharpened.

“You don’t have to like it. His job doesn’t change.”

The blonde straightened.

"I don't think it's inappropriate."

Mara stood. 

"You have a right to your opinion," she said silkily. "The more information we have on what went wrong, the better able we are to prevent problems in the future. Whether you assist us in that is up to you."

The blonde dropped the linen garment on the worktable and turned on her heels. Mara wordlessly picked up the chemise, folded it and inserted it at the bottom of the repair pile.

"Whenever you can get to it," she said to Charlie.

He nodded.

Friday afternoons became design review sessions. Mara, Charlie and I would sit at her worktable, ledger open — I would take notes. The ways garments failed had started to emerge as patterns.

Charlie usually had the floor.

Mara would say very little.

I said nothing.

We were halfway through one of these sessions that Lucy came in with the blond actress, a jacket folded over her arm. Lucy went straight to Charlie.

"I hope we're not interrupting something—"

"Carry on," said Mara.

Lucy put the jacket on the table.

"So, this isn't actually my jacket," she began when Mara interrupted her.

"Whose is it?"

Lucy startled.

"Um, well, Bree's, actually."

"Then Bree needs to bring it in."

Lucy turned the jacket over. "It's just where the sleeve's come loose—"

"Perhaps," Mara said. "We still need to know *why*. And *how*."

"But why is that so important?" the blonde interjected. "We never had to do that before."

Mara turned to Charlie.

"Show her."

Charlie swallowed, then opened the ledger.

"We're looking for failure patterns," he said quietly, not looking at anyone, "to prevent those failures in future designs."

The steamer hissed. A diesel truck rumbled by the partly open window.

"I'll ask Bree to pop 'round," Lucy said finally.

After the actresses left, Charlie glanced up. Mara tipped her chin at him.

"Carry on."

We had been back at work for only a few minutes — me at the rails, Charlie doing repairs — when the door opened briskly and a tall actress with an imposing bearing entered Wardrobe. She glanced around the room. When she found Charlie, she strode up to his table.

"Hi, I'm Bree," she said in a voice designed to carry to the budget seats.

Charlie wordlessly pulled the jacket from the pile and opened the ledger.

"May I ask what happened, please."

"Of course!"

His smile was doing its best, but his eyes looked overwhelmed. Bree was at least a foot taller than he was, with a regal bearing. I could picture her doing King Lear in the title role.

"Would you like to sit down, Bree?" he offered.

"Thought you'd never ask." She dropped herself unceremoniously into the proffered chair. I could hear it creak. "So, what do you want to know?"

Charlie consulted the ledger.

"What were you doing at the time?"

"I was thrusting with a sword."

Charlie blinked.

"I see. Could you show—"

Before he could finish, her fist flew at him, stopping inches away from his startled face. She burst into gales of laughter.

"Oh, sorry sugar, I didn't mean to..." She looked over at Mara, shaking her head and biting her lower lip. "I— I really didn't mean to—"

Mara said nothing but went back to work.

Charlie's mouth curved valiantly and so Bree's smile regained most of its cheerfulness.

"Sorry, hon," she repeated. "I tend to lose myself in the moment." She wrapped her arms around herself. "What else can I tell you?"

Charlie was carefully examining the sleeve.

"Did the jacket feel tight in the shoulders?"

Bree shook her head.

"Did it suddenly let go, or did you notice it loosening a bit at first?"

Bree's eyebrows rose.

"I didn't notice. Sorry."

Charlie turned the jacket inside out and nodded.

"I think I have what I need," he said. "I might reinforce both sleeves when I do the repair so it doesn't happen again. Is there any other issue you have with this jacket?"

Bree rose. 

"You're the talk of the Faire," she said to Charlie, who gaped at her. "Keep up the good work!"

===

A couple of days later, Lily showed up at around two in the afternoon with a rather well-nourished lass with a dour expression holding a bodice. Lily was carrying a bag from the Faire bakery.

"Afternoon tea for my favourite people in the world," she announced cheerfully.

"Lovely. Thank you," Mara said, indicating the tearoom. The dour-faced young woman found Charlie at a sewing machine.

"Do you even know how to use that thing?" she asked.

To my surprise, he looked unfazed.

"Yes, I do," he replied. "How can I help you?"

He calmly took down details about the bodice — when, where, how — and then said:

"We can add a bit of reinforcing if you'd like. So it doesn't split again."

"That would be lovely," Lily interjected before the well-nourished woman could speak. "You do such lovely work, Charlie." The scowl on the other woman's face had eased a bit. "I'll be back to the stand in a tick, Vanessa," Lily continued.

Vanessa mumbled something, approximated a smile, then turned and left.

"Don't mind her," Lily said gently to Charlie. "“She's going through a horrible breakup with her boyfriend."

Charlie nodded, his eyes a question mark.

"Was there—" he began, but Lily giggled and shook her head.

"Well, no." She paused. "Well, actually, yes." She produced a pair of black leggings from her bag. "I was wondering..."

Charlie stared at the leggings.

Mara spoke clearly, without emotion.

"We don't do alterations or repairs on personal garments."

Lily's shoulders went up. She quickly picked the leggings from Charlie's hands.
 
"Oh, sorry," she murmured. "It's just— it get so cold these days in the marketplace, especially in the early mornings. But I'm sorry, I didn't mean to—"

Mara looked at the leggings: they were of a heavy stretch cotton with a soft inner layer.

"The chemise and extra petticoats don't keep you warm?"

Lily shook her head, then got up to go.

"Wait." Mara put out her hand. "Show me."

The gusset in the crotch had given way. 

She handed the leggings to Charlie.

"This is a common fail point for leggings. Any ideas on how to fix it?"

His brows narrowed as he rubbed the stretch cotton between his fingers.

"We would need this type of material," he said slowly. "I'd replace the gusset completely, and then, add a reinforcement layer on the inside."

"Do it."

She turned to Lily.

"When we find that type of cotton, we'll let you know. We will fix it."

Mara turned to go back to her worktable. Before she sat down, she said to me,

"We need to rethink the chemise for winter. And possibly, the petticoat."

Less than a week later, a new chemise design was spread out on Mara's worktable. I was on the phone to Lauren's linen supplier when Lily appeared. She went straight to Charlie.

"Celeste said you have the material."

Charlie slowly put down his work, carefully arranging everything so he could quickly pick it all back up where he'd left off.

"I do." He reached into the repair basket and produced a bag. "If you can wait, I can do the repair for you and give them back to you straight away."

"Oh, that would be amazing!" She handed him the leggings almost reluctantly. "They're my favourite pair," she said, her eyes fixed on the garment.

"I'll be careful."

"Oh, I'm sure you are!" she breathed. "Your hands are so gentle. They remind me of Bree's."

Her face reddened suddenly.

Charlie gave her a crooked smile, then slowly started unpicking the gusset. She watched him work, the side of her lower lip gripped between her teeth. Every now and then, he would glance at her with a quick smile, then return to his work. When the old piece finally came free, he laid it over the new cotton and began marking around it.

“You're replacing the whole thing?”

“Yes. It failed.”

Lily smiled. “Right.”

When they got to the overlocker Lily said,

"I'm afraid your coffee has gone cold."

He shrugged.

"That's okay. It was nice of you to bring afternoon tea."

After Lily left, Mara pointed at the ledger.

"Just a quick note: what you repaired, and why."

Charlie turned his head at her with a slight frown.

"Why. Because the gusset was coming apart?"

"No, because the chemise and the petticoats are not keeping the actresses warm."

Charlie opened the ledger.

===

That evening, Mara had just turned off the steamer when the door opened. Bree sauntered in followed by the tall blonde, who cast a suspicious eye at Charlie almost immediately. Wearing a broad smile, Bree came up to Charlie and wrapped her arm around his shoulders. His eyes went saucer-shaped.

"I just wanted to say thank you for fixing Lily's leggings," she said cheerfully. "Those mornings in the marketplaces are getting colder all the time."

Charlie did his best. He looked up at her, curving his lips.

"Your jacket should be ready tomorrow. Was there anything else?"

Bree chuckled.

"No, hun, just wanted to say thanks. Oh, and Sarah's chemise," she said, indicating the scowling blonde beside her. "Any idea when it might be ready?"

Charlie swallowed and glanced at Mara, who had pulled the chemise from the bottom of the pile.

"Care to tell us what the issue is?" she said.

Charlie grabbed his notebook and pen, and perched attentively on the wooden stool.

Sarah huffed.

"Really?"

Mara didn't blink.

"It would help. Charlie hasn't found any signs of strain or tears."

Sarah's scowl deepened. She glanced at Charlie's face, and let out a sigh.

"It— binds."

Mara waited. Sarah let out a bigger sigh.

"Across the bust," she said finally.

Charlie was still writing when he asked:

"Would the next size up be more comfortable?"

He looked up then. Sarah was glaring at him.

"Oi! Girlfriend! Relax." Bree's voice bounced off the wall. Sarah's demeanour didn't change.

"Are you saying I'm—"

"Charlie is asking if the next size up would be more comfortable," Mara said evenly. "We can then make it more flattering if you want. A chemise should not bind."

Sarah's fists slowly unclenched.

"Sorry, I'm just not used to some strange male—"

Her body swayed suddenly as Bree gave her a shove.

"C'mon, Sarah! Charlie's one of us," Bree boomed. "Give it a rest, will ya?"

Sarah shrugged,

Mara studied the chemise for a moment, then handed it to Charlie.

"One size up?"

Charlie nodded.

"And darts."

His eyes flicked at Sarah, then back to the chemise. Bree sidled up to him.

"Show me what you would do, Charlie."

He gently smoothed the garment on its side on the table.

"Just here, Bree." His fingers move respectfully over the material. "It would give the chemise a nice silhouette, but without binding or bunching."

Sarah studied him. Her face had lost some of its hardness.

Two days later, Bree collected her jacket and Sarah's chemise.

"Oh, that feels so much better," Bree exclaimed, pulling the jacket on over her hoodie. "You're a magician, Charlie."

Charlie's smile went a bit crooked. I could see he was eyeing seams nervously.

They held.

"Now, if only you could work your magic on Lily," Bree said.

Charlie's mouth fell open.

"Did the leggings fail again?"

Bree chortled.

"No, silly, I meant her heart."

Charlie sat still for a moment.

"What do you mean? She already really likes you."

At first, Bree's eyes popped wide in surprise. She gave her head a disbelieving shake.

"Nah!"

Charlie frowned.

"Yes. She does," he said seriously. He flinched as Bree slapped him briskly on the shoulder.

"You better be right," she said as she left.

The door slammed.

Charlie flicked a pinched look at me. I suppressed a grin and went over to where Mara was pinning the new chemise on the mannequin. The material was a heavier, yet softer linen.

And the ledger sat confidently between pincushion and shears, vindicated.
