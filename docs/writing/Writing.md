## No New Tasks

[ Lauren ]

When I stepped into Celeste and Charlotte's apartment that afternoon, I noticed there was nowhere to put my keys. There was a spot for Charlotte's shoes, and Celeste's coat, but, unlike at Sarah's, no dish or hooks for keys.

*Perhaps they just kept them in their purses.* 

Even though Charlotte didn't drive, surely she held a set of keys when she had to walk home from the bus.

Surely.

I heard the click of a kettle being switched in in the kitchen, and then, Celeste appeared. She smiled, ran up to me, and threw her arms around me. I felt something inside me melt. I felt my eyes go a bit wet.

"Thank you for coming, Lauren," she whispered.

I stepped back and grasped her warm hands in mine. 

When the kettle announced it was ready to make tea, I put my thoughts on hold. My throat was making it difficult to speak anyway.

"I'm not sure where to even start," Celeste said as she set a steaming cup and urn of milk in front of me before settling into her chair. "It seems involving you in this is unfair to you."

I startled.

"How so?"

Celeste's eyes were dark.

"Well, I can't even *begin* to imagine what it would be like as a mother to watch your child have this sort of experience. I struggled a lot watching Roger realise he'd lost his child, that day he showed up at Wardrobe. And that would be *nothing* compared to what you've dealt with."

My eyes were fixed on my cup. We sat in silence for a moment.

"I won't say it was easy," I said quietly. "Nothing prepares you for that sort of thing. Not really."

She nodded, lips squeezed tightly.

"It's just— no one else in the world could possibly help me understand what to do," she said.

I waited.

Celeste studied her cup for a moment.

"The other day, Charlotte said something to me I didn't really sort of get at first. She said: 'I don't know how to be.'" Her eyes went even darker. "I didn't make much of it at the time." 

"'I don't know how to be,'" I repeated with a frown.

Celeste nodded her head slowly. She put her elbows on the table and her chin in her fists.

"I just told her to pretend she was explaining a seam, Lauren." She gave a quick puff. "She was wearing her new jumper and a different lippie. I figured she was nervous about sending signals she wasn't ready to transmit."

"Perhaps."

Celeste sat back. She rubbed the back of one hand with her fingers.

"Yeah, I don't think so either. Charlotte doesn't think that way."

"What, in terms of signals?" I said.

"In terms of how she comes across," she said evenly. "When I first met her, it was all about being unobtrusive, not getting in anyone's way. Her idea of safety was making sure no one noticed her."

I closed my eyes and put my forehead in my hands. I heard the scrape of a chair and then, felt arms around me. A tear dropped from my cheek unbidden.

"I'm sorry," I heard her whisper in my ear. "I didn't mean to—"

I shook my head vehemently.

"Please, no, Celeste. I want to know." I swallowed, my throat dry.

My cup swam before my eyes. I felt her arms press into me and melted into her.

A long moment passed.

"Look, I didn't see her either, not at first," she said finally as she regained her chair. "And she was in my maths class. Then, one day, we did Group Task." 

She suddenly fell back into her chair, her jaw slack.

"Task."

"Task?"

She nodded slowly, a frown on her face.

"Charlotte said to me, something like: 'you always seem so in control. How do you do that?'" 

Celeste stared at me, lips squeezed together.

I tipped my head.

"And if you put that together with—"

"Exactly," she said quickly. "'*I don't know how to be.*' That has nothing to do with jumpers or lipstick."

I turned my cup in my hands, feeling comfort from its warmth. I thought of a small child—my darling, the one I adored—doing. Sorting, arranging, tidying. Following me around, watching me. So keen to do things I did. So eager to learn how to sew.

At the time, it was just— my child... doing. 

I pressed my fist against my mouth. My eyes met Celeste's.

*How to say this.*

"I saw a little boy. He followed me everywhere. He was so precious, so— different, I guess. But I didn't realise it back then, when he was little." The picture in the frames on the mantelpiece were mostly of the two of us: only one included Roger. "I remember getting an invitation to a local Playgroup which Charles was three and Roger told me it wasn't good for his son to play with girls." My lips moved to one side. "I don't think it was about his son, to be honest. But I always did what I was told back then."

I stopped.

How was I going to explain to Celeste the avalanche of tears, the heartbreaking pleading, and finally, that look of betrayal in that little boy's eyes that first day I took him to school? 

That look didn't change for such a long time. 

And then, one day, it was replaced by something else.

At the time, I thought it was a rite of passage, just something every child went through.

And, at the point in time, I found out about that Roger was having an affair.

"Lauren?"

I twitched.

"Sorry, just remembering things."

"Not very pleasant, judging by the look on your face."

"No, they weren't," I admitted. "And now, it all seems much darker."

Celeste's shoulders drew in, much as I'd seen Charlotte's do in times past. I extended my hand towards her.

"Please, Celeste. I want to understand."

Her face softened.

"I think that I've been doing things wrong." She shook her head. "I kept talking to her about rungs."

"Tasks."

"Exactly." She sighed. "She doesn't need another task."

The setting sun gave the apartment a warm hue. Outside the window, the soft whirr of an electric car ended with the crack of a twig under its tyres.

I'd never wondered how that little boy had reconciled being left behind in a strange place, until now. The strange place was safe, I'd told myself back then.

"I wonder if she feels safe."

Celeste's eye snapped to mine.

"Sorry?"

"When your mum first took you to school," I said, "do you remember how you felt?"

She shrugged.

"No idea. Why?"

I wasn't letting this go.

"Let me ask you this. Did your mum and dad have people over?"

Celeste grinned.

"All the time. I used to boss their kids around. I remember my mum making me apologise to one of the boys because I made him cry."

I bit my lip to keep from smiling.

"So you liked school."

She nodded, her look sharpening.

"I don't think it's as simple as—" she began.

"I don't either," I said hurriedly. "But it could be part of it. You see," I put my cup down and leaned forward, "your path was different to Charlotte's, and not just because you were born a girl."

"So, where do we go from here, Lauren?"

I sighed.

"I wish I knew."

Celeste rose. She thoughtfully picked up our cups.

I looked up at her.

"Charlotte must think you've— arrived," I said.

"Yeah."

She turned and went into the kitchenette. I heard running water, and the door of the fridge opening.

"Would you like to stay for dinner? We're doing lasagna tonight. We've got heaps."

She came to the door. 

"Sarah's making her world-famous garlic mash tonight," I said. "Thanks anyway." I checked my phone. "I should probably get going."

Celeste came up to me and reaching out grasped my hands.

"I have something I can't give her," she said gently. "I enjoyed living, while she perfected the art of disappearing. Even though she no longer disappears, she still needs to find herself."

"She won't find it in some task," I said.

Celeste's face was solemn.

"Perhaps, in Ballarat?"

"Perhaps."
