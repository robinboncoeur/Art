# Charlie And Celeste

<hr class="section-break strong" />




## Getting Creative

[ *Celeste* ]

Charlie's damp hair cascaded down his back.

"You'll want to dry your hair a bit more, Charlie."

"Really?"

"Just the bottom half."

As the dryer blew his hair about, I put mine up.

A French-twist roll is one of the simplest hairstyles ever. 

A few twists, tuck and pin. 

Done.

I grinned at the surprised look on Charlie's face.

"How did you do that so quickly?"

I shrugged.

"Practice?" He switched off the hairdryer as I pulled the pins out. "Now, you do it." I twisted away so my back was to him. "Do what I do." I popped a hairpin between my lips and reached up to grasp my hair.

And there he was: Charlie of Wardrobe. In the mirror I watched his hands mirror mine. 

Right down to the hairpin pinched between his lips.

"That's it. Now, tuck," I said out of the corner of my mouth.

His head tilted sideways to see the back of mine, slender arms in the air, hair in a vise-like grip.

"Charlie, that's your *hair*, not Graham's ladder."

"Oh, sorry—"

His fingers began to relax their grip, then froze as a few strands fell away.

"You want it to look relaxed. Casual." I removed the hairpin from my lips. "Not maid of honour."

He flushed a bit as his eyes returned to the mirror.

"Okay, now turn it... and turn it again. That's it."

I angled my pin into the roll.

"Start at the bottom."

He winced as the first pin grazed his scalp.

"Careful. They bite." I grinned as he grabbed another pin.

He hesitated.

"About a quarter of the way up."

When he had placed the last pin, he turned to me, eyes questioning.

I pointed at the mirror.

"Have a look?"

He turned, stared, and slowly his mouth fell open.

And his eyes darted to me.

I smiled.

"What do you think?"

He frowned, his head tilting slightly.

"I was going to ask *you*," he murmured.

I snorted.

"It doesn't matter what *I* think, Charlie."

"Yes it does." He held me in his gaze. "Do you think I look stupid?"

"No." 

He turned back to the mirror.

I sat down at the table and leaned my chin into my fist.

"The real question is this: do *you* like it, Charlie?"

After a moment, he nodded slowly.

"Yes." His eyes were fixed on the mirror.

"Cool." I unplugged the hairdryer. "Think you can put it up by yourself in the morning?"

He turned to face me then, his face pale.

"Um, I'm not sure—" He paused.

I waited.

His hands went to his cheeks as he looked at his reflection in the mirror again.

I left to go to the bathroom.

He was still staring at himself in the mirror when I returned.

"It looks really nice, Charlie."

His head dipped slightly, as if agreeing with me cost him something.

"You don't seem sure."

He twitched.

"Well—"

*Right then.*

"How about doing something with the ponytail?"

"Like?"

"A loose braid on the sides?"

He reached up and pulled the pins out.

---

The next morning, he wore his hair in loose braids ending in a ponytail.

"Do I look okay?"

"Yes, Charlie."

He stared down the road, his face expressionless.

As we entered Wardrobe, I caught him glancing around the room. Mara nodded at me as we entered. Tahlia didn't look up from her overlocker. Lucy was busy pinning a sleeve on a new palace gown. Sarah was focused steaming a prototype woollen skirt.

[![Carlie and the ledger][u32a]{ .artR width="400" }][u32a]

Charlie disappeared in the repair corner with his ledger. 

Lauren came in later that morning with a cheery "hello girls" and morning tea. I noticed Charlie stiffen. Lauren gave him a quick smile and went over to where Sarah had finished with the last of the shopkeeper's dresses.

"I brought you an iced latte," she said.

"Thank you, lovely." Sarah stretched, curled up the steamer hose and followed Lauren to the tearoom.

Charlie went back to his ledger.

Later that afternoon, Leah came in from her meeting with the actresses at the palace. She winked at Charlie.

"Like the braids," she said, and then went over to Mara to report.

I could see him redden the slightest bit.

At closing, Lauren sidled up to him.

"Nice to see you doing a bit of grooming, Charlie."

He smiled but his lips were not committed.

He was quiet on the drive home.

---

He did his hair in braids again the next morning. He wore a t-shirt I hadn't seen before, brand-new judging from the creases.

"Nice t-shirt. What colour is that?"

He glanced at the shop tag he'd just removed. Turned it over.

"Um, 'Twilight Lavender'?"

"It suits you, Charlie." I smiled. "Makes a change from your usual beige and grey."

Once we got to Wardrobe, he scurried into the tearoom to put away his messenger bag like a possum escaping a goanna. And barely came out from his repair corner all day.

He'd just finished scrubbing the potatoes that evening and was stabbing them with a steak knife when he said,

"I guess I need to learn confidence." 

His voice was quiet.

I pulled a blob of mince mix out of the bowl and began to shape it into a ball.

"It'll come, Charlie." I pressed the ball between my palms, flattening it. "Remember that day Mara had you interview actresses at the palace?"

Charlie opened the microwave and placed the potatoes inside.

"I was petrified."

The microwave beeped nine times in rapid succession.

"Yeah, you were." I carefully placed the mince ball on the baking paper as the microwave started up. "I remember, your hands were shaking."

"They weren't!"

"Oh, yes they *were*." I grinned and pulled another chunk of mince mix from the bowl."But you got over it."

He shrugged. 

"Yeah, but this is different, Celeste."

"How so?"

"Well, it's not about work."

My shoulders went up slightly.

*Fair enough.*

"Can you pretend it is?"

He stopped. Tiny frowns and fleeting grimaces chased each other across his face.

"Pretend."

"That's what *I* do when I'm not sure."

His look went incredulous.

"Nah!"  

He seemed to be absorbing 'pretend' like he'd never heard it before. Finally, he pinched his lips together.

"So, you're telling me—"

"Yep." I slapped the patty onto the wax paper. "I used to pretend a fair bit." I pushed the patty flat, and tried to shape the edges. Failed. "Then, after a while, I didn't have to."

"A while?"

"A while."

I couldn't help him more than that.

---

Friday had become our fish-n-chips night. As we got into the car, dinner in hand, Charlie said,

"You were right."

I looked over.

"About?"

"Pretending."

The turn signal clicked as I turned south onto our street.

"I mean," he continued finally, "It's getting easier." He cleared his throat. "At work, I mean."

We turned into our driveway.

"How so?"

"Going to work is — different now." He puffed. "Easier." 

I unlocked the door and gave it a push with my shoulder.

It scraped on the floor.

Charlie went into the kitchenette and got out plates and cutlery. I peeled back the sticky-tape holding the wrapping paper.

"Tomato sauce or mayo?"

"Mayo."

He nodded. His face went serious as he sat down.

"It just seems no one cares. At work, I mean."

I studied my calamari rings. They sat impassively on my plate.

I thought back to year eight. Mum was teaching me table manners — specifically, where knife and fork and spoon go on a table setting and how to use them. And then, we had this Japanese exchange student staying with us for a bit. She showed me how to use chopsticks. 

I remember watching her eat. 

She would hold her bowl close to her mouth, her chopsticks flicking back and forth between the bowl and her lips. I remember thinking how cool it was that my Mum was fine with her holding her bowl like that, and also that I could never be that good. Over time, I did learn to use chopsticks — not skillfully, but confidently nevertheless.

I still had no idea how to explain confidence to Charlie.

<hr class="section-break strong" />







Mum came over to my repair corner one late January afternoon. It was beastly, really hot and sticky, even with the window open and a breeze blowing through. Sarah had finally given up steaming garments and was actually sitting with Tahlia learning about the overlocker.

"I just wanted you to know that I think you've really made a nice change to your appearance, Charlie," she said softly. I could feel my cheeks go a bit hot.

"Aw Mum," I started, but she broke in.

"Sarah noticed too." Her smile went wide. "Big improvement! Her words, Charlie."









## Chignon

[ *Charlie* ]

I stared at my reflection in the mirror.

My reflection stared back at me.

Disbelief.

Celeste had gone to bed an hour earlier. I was in the same place she'd left me.

I could see my eyes: they were on my hair.

And then, on my face.

Puzzled.

I really truly don't think that I ever, ever had looked at myself that closely, that long, in my entire life, all together, ever. Never. I mean, if you added up all the minutes I'd ever looked at myself in the mirror ever since I was born, that night would have been heaps longer. By a long shot.

I couldn't get my head around what I was seeing.

I didn't know the person in the mirror.

It was someone else.

Yet, it was me.

I'd nod.

It nodded.

I would smile.

It would smile right back.

I really liked what I was seeing in the mirror.

I didn't want it to go away, or to go back to what I was used to seeing.

I didn't want to go to bed.

But I had to.

Bed.

## Which Is It

[ *Charlie* ]

Everything had changed at Wardrobe.

Or so it seemed. I mean, it was still the same place. Still had that smell of lanolin, of chalk dust, of the soaps in the laundry and faintly of someone's perfume, perhaps Lily's. The wind was sometimes kind and pushed the porta-pottie smell towards the road, and then, sometimes not.

But now, there was a new smell.

Me.

Celeste's perfume was J'Adore by Dior. ALways. It was her.

She'd tried another scent once, a little sampler, which she then gave to me.

Matière Première. Cologne Cèdrat.

"Hold out your hand," she said that morning as we were getting ready to leave for work.

I did.

She sprayed — two little spritzes — on my left wrist.

"*Don't* rub your wrists together," she said firmly. "Stupid thing to do. It kills the scent's ability to burn down."

I stood quietly, feeling it slowly dry on my skin.

She took my hand and lifted my wrist to her nose.

"This perfume was *meant* for you!"

She guided my wrist to my nose.

And my day was just— different from that moment on.

The way I stitched, the way I touched cloth, the way I ate, even.

I felt like a different person. 

I was a person I really liked being.

I was so wrapped up in this newness — just feeling beautiful, such a soft, lovely way of being — that I never even considered what others might think.

Not until morning tea, anyway.

And then, I noticed Sarah.

She wore this really odd smile. I smiled back, and her smile broadened.

I nibbled on my sandwich, slowly. Normally, I would have devoured it in a few bites, but it seemed wrong, now. Mum came into the tearoom then, and the temperature changed. Just a bit.

They sat together, chattering about the next shipment, but there was something more, something not being said out loud, and yet that something was as plain as day. Mum was happy, relaxed, and leaning into Sarah's space as much as Sarah was enveloping Mum's.

---

I'd just finished cleaning up after dinner that evening when Celeste appeared.

She beamed at the spotless kitchenette.

"Aren't you just the ideal wife," she said teasingly. I felt like melting, her words, her smile, her warmth felt so good.

And then, suddenly, had thoughts. Words appeared in my head that left me cold. I folded the tea towel and hung it to dry, and sat down listless on the couch.

"You okay?"

She sat down next to me, close enough so I could feel her warmth. Her eyes finally found mine.

"I'm not sure what to think."

"About what?"

"About... who I am."

The cold seemed to spread.

"It's whoever you allow yourself to be, Charlie."

*Allow.*

I had no idea what to make of that word.

I'd never considered that perhaps, I was not allowing myself to... something. 

*Be?*

I was pushing at an emotional window and finding it was actually open. 

Which was frightening. 

It really shouldn't be.

At all.

*Who was I?*

I thought of how I felt that day: those Cèdrat notes kept bubbling up such happiness. 

*Why not?*

Celeste was watching me.

I leaned forward.

Ventured:

"Celeste?"

"Yes?"

I swallowed. Hard. I pulled in a breath, made as if to speak.

Stopped.

Tried again.

Stopped again.

Swallowed again.

Celeste was still watching me.

"Celeste," I finally managed, my voice a bit hoarse. "I was— happy." I licked my dry lips. "Today."

"I know."

"I don't think I was ever this happy. I never thought I *could* ever be that happy. I—"

Celeste's eyes never left mine. Her face was expressionless.

"You really want this, don't you?"

Something in me went very still.

Her words sounded like they were hiding a warning.

"Is that a bad thing?"

"Neither good or bad," she replied. "But don't be fooled: it's not all ribbons and bows."

"What do you mean?"

It was getting dark. The sheer curtain swayed as a dog barked in the distance. An aeroplane droned overhead. My right knee was dancing a little jig on its own. I tried to still it but it wouldn't listen.

"I wouldn't let one day's experience make the decision for you."

"I haven't 'decided' anything," I said.

"I could be wrong, but I think you *have* decided."

My right leg was driving me nuts — it had a way of getting me in trouble when it did its little jig.
