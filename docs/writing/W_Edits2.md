# Charlie And Celeste



<hr class="section-break strong" />



## What I Don't Want

[ *Charlie* ]

I sort of didn't tell Celeste the actual truth when she asked about my family doctor and I told her I didn't have one. Mum used to take me to see Dr Harwood in Lara every so often, not because she felt there was something that needed looking at but just to shut Dad up. Dad was forever going on about how I need to bulk up a bit and that I wasn't going to amount to anything because I couldn't stand up to the footy boys when they were having a go at me. 

Dr Harwood had suggested doing workouts at a gym. 

However, I hated the gym: it stank of unwashed bodies and sweaty rubber and smelly socks. I went a grand total of twice: the second time I clumsily dropped a weight on my foot — it had slipped off the barbell. My foot swelled up sp bad I couldn't even wear shoes for a bit. 

And limped for a month after that.

Nothing more was said about gyms.

Mara gave me the day off to go to Brunswick, which was near Melbourne. My appointment wasn't until the afternoon — good job it wasn't, because the bus trip was over three hours. One way.

Turns out Dr Winholmes wasn't in that day. I was led into the room that smelled of garlic revisited.

"Come-in, come-in, mate!"

A guy in a Queen t-shirt — Queen: the music group, not royalty — and skinny jeans beckoned to me from behind his desk. He had clearly gotten his money's worth from his gym membership.

I approached cautiously.

He indicated a chair.

I sat.

He attempted a disarming smile. Which failed to disarm.

"Hi... Charles, is it? My name's Ash. I'm a psychiatric registrar working with Dr Winholmes. She has asked me to do an initial assessment today. Is that okay with you?"

*What if I said no?*

"Yeah-okay."

He frowned slightly as he studied the form on the desk. 

"Right. So, what made you decide to come and see Dr Winholmes?"

"Celeste's doctor."

He looked up from his form.

"I see. And why did you see Celeste's doctor?"

"Because I was taking medicine to stop my body from changing."

"Right." He cleared his throat. "So, what sorts of changes were you trying to prevent?"

I stared at my hands for a while.

"That's all right," he said carefully. "We can come back to that. What medicine were you taking?"

"Spironolactone."

"Okay." He scribbled on his paper for a moment, then sat back in his chair, leaning his cheek against his fist as he studied me. "Are you still taking Spironolactone?"

I shook my head.

"Celeste's doctor put me on some other stuff."

He nodded slowly.

"Who is Celeste?"

I felt my face heat up.

"My — roomie."

The room was silent except for the pen scribbling on paper. Then:

"Are you working?"

"Yes, at Wardrobe."

"Wardrobe? What's that?"

"We maintain costumes for the Historical Faire at Anglesea."

"Wow, really?" Ash's smile was a lot more real. "I was there last weekend with my partner. Those costumes are next level!"

I gave what I hoped was a nice smile. And then:

"What is your role at Wardrobe?"

"Repairs. And testing."

"Testing?"

I felt the knot in my stomach ease a bit.

"Yeah, we're making some new costumes. My boss is very clever. She is a costume designer."

"You enjoy your work?"

I nodded, and watched him scribble some more.

"Can you describe to me what you do when you test a new garment?"

By the time I'd finished explaining what I did, the knot was almost gone.

"Sounds like you have an important position there at Wardrobe. Good people?"

I smiled.

"Best place in the world to work. Best place in the world to work."

"What would you say makes it that way?"

I thought a bit.

"I feel like I— um, fit in." I was babbling, now. "Like, I'm part of the team. They don't seem to mind I'm a — bloke."

That last bit felt like it was taking teeth with it as it came out of my mouth.

Ash put his pen down.

"May I ask you something, Charles?"

I hesitated a moment, then nodded.

"You said that your co-workers don't seem to have a problem with you being a bloke. I take it they're all women?"

"Yes."

"But do *you* mind?"

I looked at him. Speechless.

The knot came back suddenly.

He waited.

"Well, I don't want to look like one."

"All right. I think that something you and Dr Winholmes can talk about, Charles."

I felt frozen in my chair. I watched him type something into his PC, then heard his printer wake up.

"The lab work your doctor did was important." He pulled a form from the printer, scribbled on it and handed it to me. "However, we will need you to do another blood test before you see Dr Winholmes. And let's set up your next appointment, shall we?"

It was a long bus ride home.

## Complex, Not Complicated

[ *Celeste* ]

Charlie got back very late that night. He threw me a pinched smile as he pulled off his sneakers.

"How did you go?"

He shrugged as he settled into his chair, dropping his messenger bag at his feet.

"Yeah-alright."

I glanced at the clock in the kitchenette.

"Eight forty-five. You're spending your life on a bus."

"I don't mind," he murmured.

"Well, *I* mind," I said carefully. "You coming home in the dark, that's just not on. Seriously, why don't you let me take you?"

The look in his eyes reminded me of those doe eyes of his looking up to meet mine in the girl's bathroom a year ago.

"It's just, I hate to inconvenience you," he said softly.

"But you're okay with me *worrying* about you instead?"

His eyes went round.

*Oh.*

He bit his lip and his right shoulder rose a fraction.

"You hungry?"

He nodded.

"Okay with sandwiches?"

We ate in silence for a while.

"I was thinking about all this weird stuff on the bus home," he said finally.

"Oh? Like what?"

Charlie stared at his empty plate for a moment. Then, his eyebrows rose.

"Well, like, why am I so freaked out by hair growing on my legs."

"Did you tell the doctor that?"

He tipped his head.

"No." He frowned slightly. "It wasn't Dr Winholmes, just this dude who smelled of garlic."

"Smelled of garlic? How close were you sitting to him?"

Charlie grimaced.

"I wasn't close. He was sitting at his desk, asking me all these questions. And he was writing stuff, even if I didn't have an answer."

"No answer can be an answer too, Charlie."

"I know. But I just didn't know what to tell him."

"Look, you don't have to tell anyone *anything* you don't want to. Anyone. Including me."

"Oh, but no, I *want* to tell you," he blurted out. 

I waited.

"I just— don't really *know* what I want," he said finally. "I definitely *don't* want to become like my dad."

"You told him that?"

He shook his head, his lips pinched sideways, as he pulled out a stapled-together set of papers from his bag and folded the first page over. I held out my hand.

"Have a look?"

It was a pathology request form. I could make out the words 'oestrogen' and 'testosterone'. I glanced at his face. 

"I reckon I'm a bit complicated," he said quietly.

"Charlie, you're *complex*, not *complicated*," I said firmly. "That's not unusual: we're all a bit complex."

His shoulders seemed to ease slightly. His gaze drifted up to my hair. I saw his mouth soften.

I felt suddenly self-conscious.

"What?"

He looked away quickly.

"Sorry." The word was barely audible.

"No, tell me."

His cheeks went pink.

"Your hair," he said.

"What about it?"

"It's so— pretty."

I snorted.

"Tap-water straight. Hardly pretty."

"No, it's how you— um, put it up. It just looks so, um, smart."

"You like how I style my hair?"

He flicked me a shy glance and a quick nod.

"I know this sounds weird, but I feel almost, like, envious or something."

Charlie's face had gone completely pink.

"Why do you think that's weird?"

"Don't you?"

"No, I don't." I leaned back in my chair. "*I'm* envious of girls with naturally wavy hair."

"Yeah, but that's different."

"How so?"

I heard a short puff of annoyance.

"You know!"

I shook my head.

"No, I don't know." 

His shoulders rose.

"You're envious of *girls*!"

"Yeah, so? Don't tell me you've never been envious of a girl. I mean, before today."

"Not of her *looks*."

I eyed him.

"You sure?"

"Celeste!"

"Just sayin'." I got up. "So, when's your next appointment?"

"Next Thursday. At four-thirty."

"Right!" I faced him and said firmly, "Well, there's no way you're taking the bus next Thursday, Charlie."

He tipped his head.

## Filigree

[ *Charlie* ]

There was only one item on the repair pile that afternoon, a palace gown with a loose bit of lace trim on a sleeve. The lace was hanging on by a few threads. It wasn't one of the Wardrobe gowns: none of them ever lost shaping or trim or anything.

I rubbed the lace cautiously between my fingers.

And for a weird reason, flashed on Ash's Queen t-shirt.

"For instance, I really enjoy working out," I remember him telling me. And I remember his biceps flexing under his shirt. And then he said in an oddly gentle voice, "That doesn't mean everyone does. Or has to."

I gave the lace a slight tug. It unravelled further.

In our lace drawer, I found some lace on a roll of a similar design. I could feel Mara's eyes on me. Her eyebrows rose slightly.

It was her version of: 'carry on'.

I sat down and draped the dress over my knees. The smell of a hot iron caught my attention. Leah was standing over a the caraco jacket staring in disbelief at the bottom of an iron in her hand. 

"Too hot?"

Leah glanced at me.

"It wasn't working at all before, so I turned it up."

"Is there water in the thingie?"

Leah seemed to sag a bit. Her lips pinched a bit as she shook her head.

"I think I might have burnt the collar—"

"Have a look?"

She unplugged the iron and brought the jacket over, settling into a chair next to me. The hot iron smell mixed with something nicer. Bergamot. And vanilla.

"You should keep playing your oboe," she said softly, her eyes flicking over to Mara.

"I suck at the oboe."

"I know." Her lips curled slightly. "Still. Don't give it up. You don't know how good you could be if you give up straight-away."

I remember she was an amazing clarinet player: in fact, she was so good she'd played a solo piece in the school orchestra. I remember the piece: the second movement of Finzi's Clarinet Concerto.

I'd been hearing that clarinet in my head the last few days.

I put down the lace and the dress, and examined the collar.

It was scorched. 

I began to slowly unpick it.

Leah was studying my hands as I worked.

I gave her a quick smile.

And happened to notice that her hair was up in one of those 'French girl' updos.

Leah noticed.

"What?"

She frowned.

I looked away quickly.

"Sorry."

"What were you looking at?"

I felt heat rise in my face.

"Your hair."

Her frown went sideways.

"What about my hair?"

I swallowed. Hard. 

*Time to be truthful.*

"It's really pretty."

Her face went blank.

"Um, thanks." 

A short pause. 

"I guess."

Her eyes followed the *lame* in my hand. The collar was halfway off before I looked up again. 

Now, she staring at *me*.

"You should do something with *your* hair."

"What do you mean? I *do!*"

"Yeah-right, you brush it and tie it back? Whoop-dee-do."

"Back to your ironing." Mara's voice rose over the steamer. Leah gave me a look — lips squeezed, eyebrows raised — as she got up. She flounced back to her iron.

I stared at the half-removed collar.

*"That doesn't mean everyone..."*

I couldn't keep my eyes off Leah's hair. 

And caught the grin she gave me.

I grinned back.

Reflex. 

---

When I got back to the apartment, Celeste stood frowning at the open fridge.

"This is the hardest part, you know. Deciding."

"Do you want me to cook tonight?" I offered.

"Not really. You always make pasta."

"So what's wrong with pasta?"

"I'm a bit over it. I'd like to try something new."

"Something new—" I began.

And froze.

Her eyes went to me. The fridge beeped.

I shook my head.

"I— never mind," I muttered.

There was a pause. Her eyes seemed laser-focused.

The fridge beeped again.

"What were you going to say?"

"It doesn't have anything to do with food."

Her mouth tightened.

"Hair, then?"

I was suddenly intensely aware of everything: the position of my body, the look on my face. 

*How did she know?*

I suddenly felt the old fear. I could taste it — I felt myself move towards it as if it was a magnet.

"Don't."

"Don't what?"

"Don't— disappear." 

I sat down slowly, carefully, almost gingerly, as if the chair was made of crystal. 

I bit a nail.

"What were you and Leah talking about?"

"She'd scorched the collar of a jacket—"

"No," Celeste cut in. "I mean, what were you *talking* about?"

"Music."

"You were looking at *her*. It wasn't about music."

My face felt suddenly on fire. Celeste grinned.

"Yeah, I guess," I admitted.

"Are you attracted to her?"

"To Leah?" Celeste's grin broadened. The fire grew more intense. "No way!"

"You couldn't keep your eyes off her today. Everybody saw it."

*Oh shit.*

"I was just— looking at her—" I squeezed my eyes shut. "Hair."

Celeste's gaze softened.

"Ah."

*"It doesn't mean..."* 

The words sat in the air between us, words Celeste hadn't heard, but which seemed like a thin curtain.

"So, you like her hairstyle?"

I nodded. Slowly, a reluctant admission.

An flood of guilt followed. 

*I shouldn't.*

"Would you like to do your hair like that?"

The curtain whisked away, silently. The lights in the room seemed brighter, somehow. There was no point in lying. Celeste was looking right through me, just like she did in the girl's bathroom a year ago.

I hung my head, my eyes travelling to hers timidly, fearfully.

I expected mockery. Or even, a bit of teasing.

What I got was— matter-of-fact. 

"Yes or no?"

"Yes?"

"What's the big deal, Charlie?" My mouth fell open. 

Celeste seemed, sort of almost angry.

*"It doesn't..."*

"It's *your* hair, Charlie. You can do whatever you want with your hair."

"But what if I look stupid?"

Celeste's back straightened.

"Are you really worried about that? Or something else?"

She'd picked up a Vogue magazine and began flipping through it.

And stopped on a page.

For a while, she studied the images. She would look over at me, then back at the images. 

Finally, she turned the magazine around towards me.

"Here you go. Have a look. That's Leah's hairstyle, explained. Doesn't look all that hard."

I scanned the images.

*Was I actually allowed to do this?*

I looked at Celeste's face. She was studying me.

"Think you could do it?"

I shook my head.

"Why?"

"Because it's — too pretty."

*I wasn't allowed pretty, was I?*

"You're objecting to pretty? What's *wrong* with pretty? You don't like pretty?"

"No, I *like* it, but—"

"You *do* like it."

"Yes, I do."

"But."

"But... is it cool for me to do— pretty?" The last word came out in a whisper.

"It's cool for you to do anything you want with your hair."

"Even pretty?"

Yes, even pretty." She leaned forward, her eyes locked on mine. "You. Just. Do. You."

I slowly covered my mouth with both hands.

I had no idea who I was.

## The Hairbrush

[ *Celeste* ]

I was scrolling through the archives of a costume website on my phone when I noticed Charlie exit Dr Winholmes' surgery. I put my phone away as he climbed in.

"All good? How'd you go?"

Charlie flashed me a smile: a genuine one.

"She's really nice, Celeste."

"Dr Winholmes?"

"Yes." He attached his seatbelt. "She's amazing. It was so different from last week. For one thing, she smelled nice."

I grinned.

"How does she do her hair?"

"In a bob." He pursed his lips. "Her voice is so gentle, Celeste. We just sat there on her couch and had a chat. She made me feel so relaxed, like straight away."

I turned on the indicator as we joined the homeward-bound traffic.

"It's funny," Charlie continued. "Even though she was asking a lot of the same questions Ash did last week, but I didn't feel the least bit nervous around her. At all."

His fingers worried the leather strap of his messenger bag.

"I did tell her," he continued quietly, "that I envied you your hair. You know?"

"Good. What did she say?"

"She wanted to know what I liked about it."

I waited, eyebrows raised.

"Um, I said I liked how you would do different things to it, and it always looked cool." He stopped. I glanced over and saw his face had reddened slightly.

"You're blushing."

"No, I'm not."

"C'mon, tell me. What else did she say?"

"She asked me if—" 

Another pause.

"She asked you what?"

"If I'd like to do my hair like that." I could barely hear him, he spoke so quietly.

The car was quiet for a long while, the only sound being the engine doing its thing.

As we turned off the highway, he said quietly,

"I should probably wash my hair."

"Do you use conditioner?"

"No."

"You should."

He took a long time in the bathroom. He finally emerged in shorts and a t-shirt, toweling his hair vigorously.

"Charlie, what are you doing?"

His hands stopped moving.

"Drying my hair?"

"That's how to dry a St Bernard, not your hair."

"That's how I always dry my hair."

"Maybe you'd like to try something new."

His mouth went sideways.

"Okay."

I handed him my brush.

He brought it up to his head.

I shook my head.

"Start at the ends."

He was a long time brushing his hair out. It was time well spent.

He was almost finished when there was a knock on the door.

It was our neighbour, Max.

"Sorry to bother you, Celeste, but I just wanted to let you know that I'm moving out tomorrow."

"So, you took that job in Adelaide?"

Max nodded.

"Hope I'm making a good move. Anyway, you'll hear a bunch of commotion tomorrow." He held out his hand. "Just wanted to say goodbye. It's been lovely being your neighbour."

He waved to Charlie, who gave a shy wave back.

When I closed the door, I noticed Charlie had put the brush behind him.

"Why did you stop?"

Charlie reddened, and bit his lip.

"I— um—"

I tipped my chin at the brush behind his back.

"What are you doing with the brush?"

He reddened a bit more.

"I didn't want Max to see."

"See what?"

His eyes rose to mine. Imploring. Then, away.

"Charlie."

He puffed.

"Me doing this," he said quietly.

"What, brushing your hair?"

He nodded.

I stared at him for a moment.

"Okay." I looked at the brush, then him. "Do you want to finish brushing your hair?"

He swallowed and slowly resumed: careful, thoughtful strokes.

I sat down next to him.

I said nothing.

Just smiled.

## That Session

[ *Celeste* ]

I called in at the Woollies just up the road from Dr Winholmes' rooms to pick up a few things. When I returned to the car, Charlie was waiting for me.

He looked full of — *something*.

"Good session?"

His lips went thin suppressing a smile. A quick nod.

"It was."

I started the engine.

"What was the best bit?"

He spun in his seat to face me.

"It was talking about *you*."

"Me?"

"I told her that I really liked how you did your hair." He cleared his throat. "Dr Winholmes wanted to know all about it. What did I find so appealing. What was my favourite style. All that kind of stuff."

"So, all you talked about was my hair?"

"Pretty much." He attached his seatbelt. "She didn't ask any questions about me. She asked about you. And about Wardrobe."

"What did you tell her about me?"

His voice was warm: happy, even.

"That you'd found the medicine and that you asked a chemist about it. And that you'd asked your doctor to — make sure I was okay."

He fell silent. I glanced over: he was biting his lower lip.

"Anything else?"

"Um, not really." His pink cheeks told another story.

I let it go.

"What did she want to know about Wardrobe?"

"Who worked there, what I did, how did I get on with others... oh, and what was my biggest fear."

"And you said?"

"I, um." His smile faded.

We eased onto the motorway. A strong wind was buffeting the little car.

"That it was losing you as a friend."

The words sat between us as if they were glowing in neon lights. 

Neither of us spoke for a while.

He sat wringing his hands.

We rode along in silence. I couldn't find a response to that.

*A friend.*

I glanced at him and felt something tighten in my chest.

I wasn't ready for this. 

This was about *him*. 

Not *me*.

Since when did it become about *me*?

My little Swift shouldered its way through the wind down the M1.

I took in a deep breath. 

There were two truths, here. 

One: he'd been taking medication to stop becoming an adult male. He hated what was happening and took medicine to stop it.

But two, that was not his greatest fear. It was that he didn't want to lose my friendship. 

Try as I might, I couldn't get the maths to math.

The wind pushed the car around the whole time on the motorway. It wasn't until we got to Geelong that things eased a bit.

And then — suddenly — I got it.

The secrecy.

"What were you actually worried about, Charlie?"



---

---





He sat for a moment — his hands completely still.

"Yeah," he said quietly. "I just wanted to stay the way I was."

"No one just stays the way they are, Charlie."

He was silent.

I turned on the indicator and pointed the Swift towards the ocean. And home.

"I'm going to change too," I said. "It's inevitable."

"Yeah, but you'll still be—" 

He stopped.

"Be what?"

"Soft," he whispered.

*Soft.*

"Okay."

His gaze was fixed on his hands.

And then, his hand moved slowly up his arm.

I glanced at his arm. 

Even in the fading light I could tell the hair on his arm was fine, not unlike my own.

I bit my lower lip.

We turned into the driveway. I parked under the mulberry tree and turned off the engine.

And we sat for a moment.

Finally, I asked him, "Did you tell Dr Winholmes this?"

Silence.

I looked over at him. His shoulders were almost level with his ears.

"I don't know what to tell her," he mumbled. "I mean, I only know what I *don't* like."

"That's not true, Charlie," I said firmly. "You like your work in Wardrobe. You like to keep things tidy. You like my hair. And, I guess you like me."

His mouth went sideways.

"Yeah."

I opened my door.

"Let's go in."

As soon as he entered the kitchenette, he opened his messenger bag and pulled a document out of a folder. Wordlessly he handed it to me. 

There was a lot of writing. In pencil.

Words everywhere — very disjointed — not like him at all.

Some words were written hard, the pencil pushed against the paper so firmly it had formed little grooves.

Other words, written lightly, softly, almost tenderly.

I saw my name among those other words. Several times.

I looked up. His eyes were fixed on mine. Doe eyes, filled with something soft, something I saw that day in the girls' restroom, something I'd put aside then, quickly, defensively.

And now, the instinct rose to do it again.

I breathed in slowly. And looked into his eyes.

I had an idea. 

I took his arm and led him to a chair.

"Sit." 

I went to my room.

And found my hair brush.

I returned to the kitchette. 

Charlie had not moved.

"Are you okay with me touching your hair, Charlie?"

He nodded.

I felt a thud in my chest as I undid his ponytail. His hair was surprisingly fine, clean, soft. I ran my brush through it as smooth as silk. I wove the hair on each side to contain it and brought it together into a higher ponytail.

I turned to face him.

"Want to see?"

His face shone with anticipation and hope mixed with curiosity. We went to the mirror in the hall, where he stood and studied his appearance for quite some time. His eyes flicked over to me.

"Am I okay like this?"

"Do you feel okay?"

His nod seemed shy, as if reluctant to admit to anything.

"Did you enjoy having your hair brushed?"

His head bobbed again.

"Good, because you're going to brush my hair next." 

I handed him the brush.

## Which Is It

[ *Charlie* ]

Everything had changed at Wardrobe.

Or so it seemed. I mean, it was still the same place. Still had that smell of lanolin, of chalk dust, of the soaps in the laundry and faintly of someone's perfume, perhaps Lily's. The wind was sometimes kind and pushed the porta-pottie smell towards the road, and then, sometimes not.

But now, there was a new smell.

Me.

Celeste's perfume was J'Adore by Dior. ALways. It was her.

She'd tried another scent once, a little sampler, which she then gave to me.

Matière Première. Cologne Cèdrat.

"Hold out your hand," she said that morning as we were getting ready to leave for work.

I did.

She sprayed — two little spritzes — on my left wrist.

"*Don't* rub your wrists together," she said firmly. "Stupid thing to do. It kills the scent's ability to burn down."

I stood quietly, feeling it slowly dry on my skin.

She took my hand and lifted my wrist to her nose.

"This perfume was *meant* for you!"

She guided my wrist to my nose.

And my day was just— different from that moment on.

The way I stitched, the way I touched cloth, the way I ate, even.

I felt like a different person. 

I was a person I really liked being.

I was so wrapped up in this newness — this beautifulness — this soft lovely way of being that I never even considered what others might think.

Not until morning tea, anyway.

And then, I noticed Sarah.

She wore this really odd smile. I smiled back, and her smile broadened.

I nibbled on my sandwich, slowly. Normally, I would have devoured it in a few bites, but it seemed wrong, now. Mum came into the tearoom then, and the temperature changed. Just a bit.

They sat together, chattering about the next shipment, but there was something more, something not being said out loud, and yet that something was as plain as day. Mum was happy, relaxed, and leaning into Sarah's space as much as Sarah was enveloping Mum's.

---

I'd just finished cleaning up after dinner that evening when Celeste appeared.

She beamed at the spotless kitchenette.

"Aren't you just the ideal wife," she said teasingly. I felt like melting, her words, her smile, her warmth felt so good.

And then, suddenly, had thoughts. Words appeared in my head that left me cold. I folded the tea towel and hung it to dry, and sat down listless on the couch.

"You okay?"

She sat down next to me, close enough so I could feel her warmth. Her eyes finally found mine.

"I'm not sure what to think."

"About what?"

"About... who I am."

The cold seemed to spread.

"It's whoever you allow yourself to be, Charlie."

*Allow.*

I had no idea what to make of that word.

I'd never considered that perhaps, I was not allowing myself to... something. 

*Be?*

I was pushing at an emotional window and finding it was actually open. 

Which was frightening. 

It really shouldn't be.

At all.

*Who was I?*

I thought of how I felt that day: those Cèdrat notes kept bubbling up such happiness. 

*Why not?*

Celeste was watching me.

I leaned forward.

Ventured:

"Celeste?"

"Yes?"

I swallowed. Hard. I pulled in a breath, made as if to speak.

Stopped.

Tried again.

Stopped again.

Swallowed again.

Celeste was still watching me.

"Celeste," I finally managed, my voice a bit hoarse. "I was— happy." I licked my dry lips. "Today."

"I know."

"I don't think I was ever this happy. I never thought I *could* ever be that happy. I—"

Celeste's eyes never left mine. Her face was expressionless.

"You really want this, don't you?"

Something in me went very still.

Her words sounded like they were hiding a warning.

"Is that a bad thing?"

"Neither good or bad," she replied. "But don't be fooled: it's not all ribbons and bows."

"What do you mean?"

It was getting dark. The sheer curtain waved in as a dog batked in the distance. An aeroplane droned overhead. My right knee was dancing a little jig on its own. I tried to still it but it wouldn't listen.

"I wouldn't let one day's experience make the decision for you."

"I haven't 'decided' anything," I said.

"I could be wrong, but I think you *have* decided."
