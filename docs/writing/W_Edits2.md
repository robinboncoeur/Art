# Charlie And Celeste



<hr class="section-break strong" />




## 

[ *Celeste* ]

Charlie got back from Melbourne very late that night. He threw me a pinched smile as he pulled off his sneakers.

"How did you go?"

He settled into his chair, dropping his messenger bag at his feet.

"Yeah-alright." He bent over to open the front of the bag, and pulled out a stapled-together document. "A little light reading," he said as he waved it at me.

I glanced at the clock in the kitchenette.

"Eight forty-five. Are you sure I can't take you to your appointments?"

"I don't mind," he murmured.

"Well, *I* mind," I said carefully. "Coming home in the dark, that's just not on. Seriously, why don't you let me take you?"

The look in his eyes reminded me of a pair of doe eyes looking up to meet mine in the girl's bathroom a year ago.

"I don't want to inconvenience you," he said softly.

"But you're okay with me worrying about you instead?"

His eyes went round. He bit his lip and his right shoulder rose a fraction.

*Oh.*

"Hungry?"

He nodded.

"Okay with sandwiches?"

We ate in silence for a while.

"Is it okay if I ask you a few questions, Celeste?"

"Of course!"

Charlie stared at his empty plate for a moment. Then, his eyebrows rose.

"Well, I'm not sure if I'm doing it right."

"Doing what right?"

He tipped his head.

"Answering the psychologist." He frowned slightly. "I— I didn't see Dr Winholmes today. Just this dude with bad breath asked me a whole lot of questions."

"You were sitting that close you could smell his breath?"

Charlie grimaced.

"No. He sat at his desk: he was writing stuff down. And frowning a lot. And asking questions I couldn't answer."

"Not being able to answer is an answer too, Charlie."

"I know. But—"

"Look, you don't have to tell me anything. In fact—"

"No, but I want to," he blurted out. "After talking to that dude, I'm more confused than ever. All I know is, I don't want to become like my dad."

"You told him that?"

He nodded, his lips pinched sideways. "I don't think he was impressed." He folded the page over to another document. "I'm meant to see someone else before I see Dr Winholmes—" He stared at the page before him. I held out my hand.

"Have a look?"

It was a referral form. Behind it was a order for laboratory tests: I could make out the words 'oestrogen' and 'testosterone'. I glanced at his face. 

"You look stressed."

"I'm sorry," he said quietly. "I didn't mean to be complicated."

"Charlie, you're *complex*, not *complicated*," I said firmly. "Nothing wrong with that."

His shoulders seemed to ease slightly.

"It seemed like I couldn't answer any question properly."

"Maybe the questions were not the ones he should have been asking."

Charlie shrugged.

"I guess." His gaze rose slightly: he seemed to be studying my hair. His mouth softened.

I felt suddenly self-conscious.

"What are you looking at?"

He looked away quickly.

"Sorry." The word was barely audible.

"No, tell me."

His cheeks went pink.

"Your hair," he said.

"What about it?"

"It's so— pretty."

I snorted.

"Straight as tap-water. Hardly pretty."

"No, it's how you put it up. It just looks so, um, smart."

I had a crazy thought: 'we could do the same to your hair' but immediately kicked the notion to the kerb. Just the thing to confuse Charlie further.

"You like how I style my hair?"

He flicked me a shy glance and a quick nod.

"I know this sounds weird, but I'm almost, like, envious or something."

"Oh?"

"Yeah." He pursed his lips. "I actually told the dude that I liked your hair."

"What did he say?"

"Nothing. He just gave me this look, and then wrote something down. And he asked me then about my dad. Whether we have a good relationship."

His voice had gone a bit dark. I felt my spine stiffen. His mouth opened as if to speak, and then, closed it again.

"Will you be seeing that psychologist again?"

Charlie shook his head.

"I don't think so."

---

I was sitting in my little Swift outside Dr Winholmes rooms scrolling through the archives of a costume website on my phone when I noticed Charlie exit the building. I put my phone away as he climbed in.

"All good?"

Charlie flashed me a smile: a genuine, warm upward curve of the lips. He attached his seatbelt.

"Chalk and cheese."

"You saw Dr Winholmes?"

"Yes, Celeste. She's amazing. It was so different from last week. For one thing, she smelled nice."

I grinned.

"How does she do her hair?"

"In a bob." He pursed his lips. "She speaks very gently. And she doesn't sit at a desk: we sat on a couch. I felt a lot more relaxed around her."

I turned on the indicator as we joined the homeward-bound traffic.

"It's funny," Charlie continued. "She was asking a lot of the same questions as the dude last week, but I didn't feel nervous around her. At all. Even though I didn't have any more answers than I did last week, either."

His fingers worried the leather strap of his messenger bag.

"Only thing I could answer was: I know what I *don't* want. You know."

I knew.

---

Sarah and Tahlia had just finished packing up the last of the dresses and were taping the boxes when Charlie emerged from his repair corner with several lace caps.

"Were these meant to go with the dresses?"

Tahlia shook her head.

"They're not on the invoice."

Behind them, a voice boomed:

"When are you girls doing lunch?"

It was Bree. She was grinning at Charlie, who smiled back, unperturbed. Bree slipped an arm around his slender shoulders as they walked towards the tearoom, where they found Lily opening the bag from the Thai takeaway.

"Pad thai for me and for the lovely Charlie," she said cheerfully, "and a Massaman beef for the brave Bree."

Charlie reddened even as he smiled. His smile froze slightly as he caught Sarah staring at him with raised eyebrows. He focused his attention on his noodles.

"You know, there's another position opening in the palace," Bree said. 

Lily looked puzzled.

"I didn't realise any of the guys had left."

Bree pursed her lips.

"They haven't." Her eyebrows rose meaningfully as she glanced at Charlie. Lily followed her gaze, a smile flickering on her lips. 

Mara appeared at the door.

"You're not recruiting my staff, are you, Bree?"

"I would *never!*"

Mara looked over her glasses at Bree.

"That's good then."

Charlie gave no sign that he was listening but his blushes betrayed him.

---

Charlie was standing next to the Swift when I returned with the shopping from Woollies. He looked full of — something.

"Good session?"

His lips went thin suppressing a smile. A quick nod.

"It was."

I started the engine.

"What was the best bit?"

He spun in his seat to face me.

"It was about *you*."

"Me?"

Charlie seemed almost breathless.

"I told her that I really liked how you did your hair." He cleared his throat. "Dr Winholmes wanted to know all about it. What did I find so appealing. What was my favourite style. All that kind of stuff."

"So, all you talked about was my hair?"

"Yep." He attached his seatbelt. "She didn't ask any questions about me. She asked about you. And about Wardrobe."
