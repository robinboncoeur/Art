# Charlie And Celeste

<hr class="section-break strong" />




## Getting Creative

[ *Celeste* ]

Charlie's damp hair cascaded down his back.

"You'll need to dry your hair a bit more, Charlie."

His shoulders sagged a bit.

"Really?"

"Just do the bottom half."

As he dried his hair, I put mine up.

A French-twist roll is one of the simplest hairstyles ever. 

A few twists, tuck and pin. 

Done.

I grinned at the surprised look on Charlie's face.

"How did you do that so quickly?"

I shrugged.

"Been doing this for a while, Charlie." I pulled the pins out. "Now, you do it." I twisted away slightly — my back to him — so he could see. "Do what I do."

I reached up to grasp my hair.

And there he was: Charlie of Wardrobe. In the mirror I watched his hands mirror mine.

"That's it. Now, tuck."

His head tilted sideways to see the back of mine, slender arms in the air, fingers tightly gripping his hair.

"Charlie, that's your *hair*, not Graham's ladder. You've got a death-grip on it."

"Oh, sorry—"

His shoulders dropped noticeably.

His fingers relaxed their grip, then froze as a few strands fell away.

"You're going for a relaxed, casual style." I peered at him. "That's the look Leah does."

His eyes returned to his reflection in the mirror.

"Okay, now turn it... and turn it again. That's it."

I handed him a hairpin.

"Start at the bottom."

He winced as the first pin grazed his scalp.

"You'll get used to that," I said with a grin, handing him another pin.

He nodded quickly, his face expectant. 

Hesitated.

"About a quarter of the way up."

When he had placed the last pin, he turned to me, eyes questioning.

I pointed at the mirror.

"Have a look?"

He turned, stared, and slowly his mouth fell open.

And his eyes darted to me.

I smiled.

"What do you think?"

He frowned, his head tilting slightly.

"I was going to ask *you*," he murmured.

I snorted.

"It really doesn't matter what *I* think, Charlie."

"Yes it does." He held me in his gaze. "Do you think I look stupid?"

I grimaced.

"What kind of question is that?"

He turned back to the mirror.

"*Do* you?"

I sat down at the table.

"No I don't, Charlie." I leaned my chin into my fist.

He tipped his head.

*Okay.*

Turned back to the mirror.

"The real question is this: do *you* like it?"

He nodded slowly.

"Yes."

"Cool." I unplugged the hairdryer. "Think you can put it up by yourself in the morning?"

He turned to face me then, his face pale.

"Um, I'm not sure—" He paused.

I waited.

His hands went to his cheeks as he looked at the floor.

I tried not to smile.

"Too much all at one go?"

His nod was just a twitch.

"Maybe—"

"How about doing something with the ponytail?"

His eyebrows tried to hide in his forehead.

"Like?"

"Maybe— a loose braid?"

He reached up and pulled the pins out.

"Maybe."

---

The next morning, he wore his hair in loose braids ending in a ponytail.

"I'm a little unsure about this," he said in the car.

"You can always take the braids out, Charlie."

His face set as he stared down the road.

"No."

As we entered Wardrobe, I could see his eyes dart around the room. Mara nodded at me as we entered and took no notice of him at all. Tahlia didn't look up from her overlocker. Lucy eas busy pinning a sleeve on a new palace gown and Sarah focused on the steamer.

Charlie settled in the repair corner with his ledger.

When Lauren came in later that morning, Charlie visibly stiffened.

Lauren smiled at him and went over to where Sarah had finished with the last of the shopkeeper's dresses.

"I brought you a coffee," she said to Sarah, indicating the tearoom with her thumb.

"Thank you, lovely," Sarah replied as she curled up the steamer hose.

Charlie went back to his ledger.

Later that afternoon, Leah came in from her meeting at the palace. She gave Charlie a smile.

"Like the braids," she said simply and then, went over to Mara to report.

I could see him redden the slightest bit.

At closing, Lauren sidled up to him.

"Nice to see you doing a bit of grooming, Charlie," she said with a gentle smile.

He smiled back, his lips not entirely committing. 

More like a Dustin Hoffman than a Geena Davis.

He was quiet on the drive home.

---

He did his own braids the next morning.

And even selected a new t-shirt to wear, one I hadn't seen before. It was an unusual colour.

"That's new. What colour is that?"

He still had the shop tag in his hand. Turned it over.

"Um, 'Twilight Lavender'?"

"It suits you, Charlie." I smiled. "Something different to your usual beige and grey."

I could see him visibly bracing as he got out of the car at Wardrobe, but said nothing.

He scurried into the tearoom to put away his messenger bag like a tree-rat running along a fence. 

And barely came out from his repair corner all day.

I finally decided to call him on it.

"You right, there, mate?"

He looked up from the bodice he was working on.

"Yeah?"

"You don't seem so sure."

He shrugged dismissively.

"Nah-I'm-right."

He wasn't able to look at me.

The drive home was a quiet one.

He'd just finished scrubbing the potatoes and was stabbing them with a steak knife when he said, quietly,

"I guess I need to learn confidence."

I pulled a blob of mince mix out of the bowl and began to roll it betwwen my palms.

"Remember that day Mara had you interview actresses at the palace?"

His eyebrows rose slightly. He opened the microwave and placed the potatoes inside.

"I was petrified."

The microwave beeped nine times in rapid succession.

"You were." I carefully placed the mince ball on the baking paper as the microwave started up. 

And pulled another chunk of mince mix from the bowl. 

"And how long did that last?"

He shrugged. 

And grimaced.

---

Friday had become our fish-n-chips night. As we got into the car, dinner in hand, Charlie said,

"You were right."

I looked over.

"About?"

"Confidence."

The turn signal clicked as I turned south onto our street.

"I mean," he continued finally, "I guess you don't really *learn* confidence."

We turned into our driveway.

"Actually, you do," I said. "Just, not the way you think."

"How did *you* learn it?" he asked as I unlocked the door.

And gave it a push with my shoulder.

It scraped on the floor.

Charlie went into the kitchenette and got out plates and cutlery. I peeled back the sticky-tape holding the wrapping paper.

"Tomato sauce or mayo?"

"Mayo," I replied. "I'm going back to my Flemish roots."

"I didn't know you had ancestors in Belgium."

I suppressed a grin.

"I don't."

He nodded, his face serious.

"So, how did you learn confidence?"

I studied my last calimari ring as if it might hold the answer.

My calimari ring sat impassively on my plate.

I thought back to year eight. Mum was teaching me table manners — specifically, where knife and fork and spoon go on a table setting and how to use them. And then, in year eight, a Japanese girl showed me how to use chopsticks. 

I remember watching her chopsticks flick back and forth between the bowl and her lips and thinking I could never be that good.

I still use chopsticks for certain meals. Not all that well, but quite confidently.

Perhaps confidence and ability were related, but only loosely.

I still had no idea how to explain confidence to Charlie.

## Chignon

[ *Charlie* ]

I stared at my reflection in the mirror.

My reflection stared back at me.

Disbelief.

Celeste had gone to bed an hour earlier. I was in the same place she'd left me.

I could see my eyes: they were on my hair.

And then, on my face.

Puzzled.

I really truly don't think that I ever, ever had looked at myself that closely, that long, in my entire life, all together, ever. Never. I mean, if you added up all the minutes I'd ever looked at myself in the mirror ever since I was born, that night would have been heaps longer. By a long shot.

I couldn't get my head around what I was seeing.

I didn't know the person in the mirror.

It was someone else.

Yet, it was me.

I'd nod.

It nodded.

I would smile.

It would smile right back.

I really liked what I was seeing in the mirror.

I didn't want it to go away, or to go back to what I was used to seeing.

I didn't want to go to bed.

But I had to.

Bed.

## Which Is It

[ *Charlie* ]

Everything had changed at Wardrobe.

Or so it seemed. I mean, it was still the same place. Still had that smell of lanolin, of chalk dust, of the soaps in the laundry and faintly of someone's perfume, perhaps Lily's. The wind was sometimes kind and pushed the porta-pottie smell towards the road, and then, sometimes not.

But now, there was a new smell.

Me.

Celeste's perfume was J'Adore by Dior. ALways. It was her.

She'd tried another scent once, a little sampler, which she then gave to me.

Matière Première. Cologne Cèdrat.

"Hold out your hand," she said that morning as we were getting ready to leave for work.

I did.

She sprayed — two little spritzes — on my left wrist.

"*Don't* rub your wrists together," she said firmly. "Stupid thing to do. It kills the scent's ability to burn down."

I stood quietly, feeling it slowly dry on my skin.

She took my hand and lifted my wrist to her nose.

"This perfume was *meant* for you!"

She guided my wrist to my nose.

And my day was just— different from that moment on.

The way I stitched, the way I touched cloth, the way I ate, even.

I felt like a different person. 

I was a person I really liked being.

I was so wrapped up in this newness — just feeling beautiful, such a soft, lovely way of being — that I never even considered what others might think.

Not until morning tea, anyway.

And then, I noticed Sarah.

She wore this really odd smile. I smiled back, and her smile broadened.

I nibbled on my sandwich, slowly. Normally, I would have devoured it in a few bites, but it seemed wrong, now. Mum came into the tearoom then, and the temperature changed. Just a bit.

They sat together, chattering about the next shipment, but there was something more, something not being said out loud, and yet that something was as plain as day. Mum was happy, relaxed, and leaning into Sarah's space as much as Sarah was enveloping Mum's.

---

I'd just finished cleaning up after dinner that evening when Celeste appeared.

She beamed at the spotless kitchenette.

"Aren't you just the ideal wife," she said teasingly. I felt like melting, her words, her smile, her warmth felt so good.

And then, suddenly, had thoughts. Words appeared in my head that left me cold. I folded the tea towel and hung it to dry, and sat down listless on the couch.

"You okay?"

She sat down next to me, close enough so I could feel her warmth. Her eyes finally found mine.

"I'm not sure what to think."

"About what?"

"About... who I am."

The cold seemed to spread.

"It's whoever you allow yourself to be, Charlie."

*Allow.*

I had no idea what to make of that word.

I'd never considered that perhaps, I was not allowing myself to... something. 

*Be?*

I was pushing at an emotional window and finding it was actually open. 

Which was frightening. 

It really shouldn't be.

At all.

*Who was I?*

I thought of how I felt that day: those Cèdrat notes kept bubbling up such happiness. 

*Why not?*

Celeste was watching me.

I leaned forward.

Ventured:

"Celeste?"

"Yes?"

I swallowed. Hard. I pulled in a breath, made as if to speak.

Stopped.

Tried again.

Stopped again.

Swallowed again.

Celeste was still watching me.

"Celeste," I finally managed, my voice a bit hoarse. "I was— happy." I licked my dry lips. "Today."

"I know."

"I don't think I was ever this happy. I never thought I *could* ever be that happy. I—"

Celeste's eyes never left mine. Her face was expressionless.

"You really want this, don't you?"

Something in me went very still.

Her words sounded like they were hiding a warning.

"Is that a bad thing?"

"Neither good or bad," she replied. "But don't be fooled: it's not all ribbons and bows."

"What do you mean?"

It was getting dark. The sheer curtain swayed as a dog barked in the distance. An aeroplane droned overhead. My right knee was dancing a little jig on its own. I tried to still it but it wouldn't listen.

"I wouldn't let one day's experience make the decision for you."

"I haven't 'decided' anything," I said.

"I could be wrong, but I think you *have* decided."

My right leg was driving me nuts — it had a way of getting me in trouble when it did its little jig.
