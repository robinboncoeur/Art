[ *Celeste* ]

Mara put a ledger on the table one morning.

Thick. Hard cover. The kind that would you'd worry about offending if you dropped it. It claimed a spot between pincushion and shears, beside the tin of chalk, accompanied by a wooden stool that hadn't seen varnish in decades.

On the first page of the ledger Mara had already ruled headings in her angular hand.

GARMENT:  
DATE ISSUED:  
ISSUED TO:  
PROBLEM REPORTED:  
REPAIR:  
RE-TEST:

Mara tapped the headings once.

"These are not set in stone," she said. "This is a starting point."

"To catalogue repairs?"

Mara shook her head.

"No. Everything. No more mending pile."

I glanced over to the other end of the worktable, where the mending pile once lived. Charlie had just finished repairing the last item the night before. 

I was surprised to see a folded up chemise sitting on the edge, looking a bit abandoned. Mara's lips tightened.

"Who's is that?"

Charlie arrived just then, quietly. He started undoing the buttons of his jacket as he followed Mara's gaze.

"Oh, that? One of the actresses dropped it off last night, just at closing. You had just—"

"Who?"

Charlie startled slightly. He frowned, as if trying to remember.

"I think her name as Lily." He gave a nod. "Yeah, that was it."

He removed his denim jacket.

Mara picked up a spiral notebook. She fixed her gaze on his face.

"Your task this morning is to find Lily and get details on how the garment failed."

"Find her?" He looked puzzled.

"Yes. I think she works in the marketplace."

His eyes went a bit wider as she handed him the spiral notebook and the chemise. He stared at the garment.

"Um, I had a look at it last night," he said. "I could probably tell—"

"'Probably' isn't data," Mara said firmly.

His face fell. Mara saw it.

Of course she did.

"Is there a problem?"

"No-no," he said hastily. "I just thought—"

"That your testing abilities were going to answer every question?" 

She waited. 

He bit his lip, his hands shaking slightly as he slipped his jacket back on. He picked a pen out of the tin.

"Details," Mara said. "What she was doing when it failed, if it was pinching, if it was something she'd noticed was starting to fail, and any other information you can think of."

"Yes, Mara."

He glanced at me as he moved to the door.

And then, the door clicked shut. Mara's eyes met mine.

I went back to work.

Later that morning, I opened the window. A cold wind had come up and was blowing leaves around the carpark. I propped the window partly open to let in a bit of fresh air, wondering why I couldn't smell the usual hot chips porta-pottie medley.

I had just returned to invoices when the door opened.

Lucy came in, wearing a huge grin. She sidled up to me.

"Who sent Charlie out to meet the wolves?"

I tipped my chin at Mara.

"He's looking like a reporter for the local paper at a hostage scene," Lucy said, her voice lower. "Asked poor Lily a bazillion questions and then, scribbling away—"

I nodded.

"He's taking his job seriously."

Lucy gave a sharp laugh.

"Lily never knew what hit her." She tossed her head. "And then, this other actress—"

The door burst open and Charlie tottered in, eyes scanning his notes. His mouth was to one side.

Mara looked up from her work.

"Did you get it all?"

Charlie tipped his head slightly to one side.

"I think so."

Mara's eyes rested on the notebook.

"Have a look?"

His neck reddened slightly as he passed it over to her. She glanced over it, and handed it back.

"Can you read this?"

"Yes."

"Good. Transcribe it."

She pointed at the ledger.
