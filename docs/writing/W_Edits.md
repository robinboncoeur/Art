[ *Celeste* ]

**An outline.** 




*First the reader experiences the Space.* 

I got there early that cool morning, glad for my cardigan. 

The key rasped in the lock and the main door swung silently open.

The unlit workroom breathed wool and chalk dust, the faint medley of scents the women had accidentally left behind and coffee old enough to vote.

Behind me, the door clicked shut. The life outside went mute, as if covered by a pillow. As I crossed to my desk on the far side of the workroom, my footfalls seemed disrespectful of the quiet the room had watched over during the night.

A colourless, cloud-veiled sunrise waited outside the workroom window.

I switched on the overheads. 

Warm white: Mara had chosen well. Some fluorescent lights turn everything ashen, but these lights celebrated colours.

I pulled off my cardigan, sat down on the only rolling chair in the room and pulled out the folder with last night's work. Mara and I had a meeting at one pm with the Faire accountant about a billing discrepancy they'd "uncovered". I wasn't going to let Wardrobe be caught flat-footed.

The growl of a diesel engine announced someone opening the door, then stilled as the door closed with a click.

"Morning."

I turned at the unfamiliar voice. Bree, one of the Faire actresses was holding up a handbag.

"Forgot it last night. When I saw the light was on—"

I gave her a smile and a wave, and she was gone.

My nose went back into the stack of invoices, one of which smelled like Graham.

By the time Mara opened the door, I'd scarcely made it a third of the way through. She didn't go into the tearoom as she usually did, but approached me thoughtfully.

"I am grateful for your dedication to work," she said, gently, I thought, for her, "but this is my responsibility, not yours."

I glanced nervously at her.

"I wasn't trying to—"

"I know." She set her bag down and pulled up an ancient wooden chair next to me, her lips in a firm line.

"You take work seriously, and I appreciate that," she said. "Just keep it balanced. Please."

I'd never seen her eyes so warm.

"Let's get a coffee. Then, work on this. Together." She handed me my cardigan and picked up her bag.

The door opened just then and Tahlia and Lucy came in, takeaway coffees in hand, chattering like magpies. 

"You girls get the day started," Mara said. "Celeste and I won't be a minute."

By the time we got back, the steamer was hissing at a cape next to an open window. The sun had finally decided on making an appearance and was showing the fluorescent lights how it's done. Somewhere outside, a kookaburra was laughing at the murmur of voices from the Faire.

The door opened again, and Charlie slipped in.

Tahlia waved me over.

"Did we get the new bobbins for the overlocker?"

I pointed at a parcel leaning against a sewing machine.




Sound. Smells. Light. Shade. Heat. Cold. 





*Then, movement in that Space.* 

Garments on a rolling rack sway as they move through the room. 
Footfalls pushing the rolling rack, the wheels on the wooden floor. 




*To human interactions*

A little laugh. 
And then, sets of eyes. 
Mara's eyes. 
Celeste's eyes. 
Tahlia's eyes.
