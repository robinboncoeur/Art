# Edits


[ *Celeste* ]

I got there early that cool morning. 

The key rasped in the lock and the main door swung silently open.

The unlit workroom breathed wool and chalk dust blended with the faint medley of scents the women had accidentally left behind and leftover coffee old enough to vote.

Behind me, the door clicked shut. The life outside went mute, as if covered by a pillow. As I crossed to my desk on the far side of the workroom, my footfalls seemed disrespectful of the quiet the room had watched over during the night.

A colourless, cloud-veiled sunrise waited outside the workroom window.

I switched on the overheads. 

Warm white: Mara had chosen well. Some fluorescent lights turn everything ashen, but these lights celebrated colours.

I pulled off my cardigan, sat down on the only rolling chair in the workroom and pulled out the folder with last night's work. Mara and I had a meeting at one pm with the Faire accountant about a billing discrepancy the accountant had "uncovered". I wasn't going to let Wardrobe be caught flat-footed.

The growl of a diesel engine outside announced someone opening the door, then stilled as the door closed with a click.

"Morning."

I turned at the unfamiliar voice. Bree, one of the Faire actresses, was holding up a handbag.

"Forgot it last night. When I saw the light was on—"

I gave her a smile and a wave, and she was gone.

My nose went back into the stack of invoices, one of which smelled like Graham.

By the time Mara opened the door, I'd scarcely made it a third of the way through. She didn't go into the tearoom as she usually did, but approached me thoughtfully.

"I am grateful for your dedication," she said — gently, for her, "but this is my responsibility, not yours."

I glanced nervously at her.

"I wasn't trying to—"

"I know." She set her bag down and pulled up an ancient wooden chair next to mine, her lips in a firm line.

"You take work seriously. I appreciate that," she said. "Just keep it balanced. Please."

I'd never seen her eyes so warm.

"Let's get a coffee. Then, we'll work on this. Together." She handed me my cardigan and picked up her bag.

The door opened just then and Tahlia and Lucy came in, takeaway coffees in hand, chattering like magpies. 

"You girls get the day started," Mara said. "Celeste and I won't be a minute."

By the time we got back, the sun had finally decided on making an appearance and was showing the fluorescent lights how it's done. Somewhere outside, a kookaburra was laughing at the murmur of voices from the Faire. Inside, the steamer hissed at a cape near an open window, while on the other side of the room a sewing machine whirred.

The door opened again, and Charlie slipped in.

Tahlia waved me over.

"Did we get the new bobbins for the overlocker?"

I pointed at a parcel leaning against a sewing machine.

"Came in yesterday. Be sure to pass me the invoice, won't you?"

When Leah arrived, she moved towards Charlie at the mending corner, like a timid barrister approaching the bench.

"Do you still play your oboe?" she asked with a crooked smile.

He looked up briefly and shook his head. His ears had gone a bit pink.

"Leah, you're on rails," Mara said.

A smile stole across Leah's face as she removed her jacket and went to work.

That afternoon, Lucy carried a heavy, ornate dress from the storage room, awkwardly trying to keep the hem off the floor. She hung it on the tallest rail and stepped back with arms crossed, eyeing it critically.

"Lily's been assigned to the palace again," she announced to no one in particular.

Mara looked up from her work.

"It'll need going over with the steamer." She frowned. "Is that dress even going to fit her?"

The back door — a roller gate — slammed shut and Tahlia emerged with an envelope tucked under one arm, carrying a roll of hunter green fabric in the crook of her arms. Charlie jumped up but Tahlia shook her head.

"I'm good, hun," she said. She twitched the shoulder with the envelope. "Just take that invoice over to Celeste, won't you?"

He carefully slipped the envelope from under her arm.

"You might put that bolt on the cutting table," Mara told Tahlia. "Been waiting for that since last week."

Lucy had finished her inspection of the dress just as Lily, a little redhead with freckles, a cheery smile, and long eyelashes designed to invoke envy from every woman in the room bounced through the door. Her eyes widened at the sight of the dress.

Hands on hips, Lucy turned to her.

"Shall we?"

Lily carelessly began to pull her jumper off when Lucy coughed. She followed Lucy's gaze in the direction of the mending corner. 

Shrugged, and continued to pull off her jumper.

Charlie didn't look up.

The threesome — Lucy, Lily and the ornate dress — made their way to the fitting area.

I had just opened the envelope and tossed the invoice on the pile to be reviewed when I noticed Charlie's head come up. His eyes flicked from the fitting area to the open window. Wordlessly, he rose and closed the window, then disappeared again amidst torn and frayed garments.

Mara's eyes caught mine. Her lips pressed together slightly.

She'd noticed.

Of course she had.

"We'll need to take this in a bit," Lucy said as she and Lily emerged from the fitting area, Lucy holding the sides of the dress against Lily. She looked down at the little redhead. "Have you lost weight?"

"This wasn't my dress," Lily replied. "Mine wasn't this pretty."

She lifted the skirt, which was dragging on the floor.

"See? Not my dress."

Mara put down the invoice she was studying.

"Find a palace dress in storage that won't require as many alterations."

Lucy nodded and turned back to Lily, who had whipped off the dress.

"Oof. Pretty, but hot."

Lily slipped into her oversized jumper, which covered much of her black leggings.

There was a knock on the door.

"That will be the accountant. Leah, show him in, please," I said.

The temperature in the room seemed to change as Leah led a rather large, florid-faced man with thinning hair and four-day salt-and-pepper facial hair to our desk. I got up and offered him my chair, which he settled into with a grunt. His eyes travelled around the room, searching.

Lily disappeared.

Mara's eyes scanned the workroom, then landed on mine.

Tahlia glanced once in our direction, then seemed to sink out of sight behind her overlocker.

I ended up staring at the invoices with a slight feeling of revulsion not inspired by invoices.

After twenty minutes, it became clear that Mr Accountant had "uncovered" a rounding error. He harrumphed a few times and twiddled his pen, exuding a stale atmosphere of poor hygiene decisions.

The room warmed slightly as the door finally clicked shut behind his receding back.

Tahlia threw open her window.

I opened the door to the laundry. Fabric softener highlights slowly escorted stale, dour pong out the window.

Wardrobe cautiously resumed breathing wool and chalk dust.
